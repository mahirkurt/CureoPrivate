# Competitive Audit Protocol

Mandatory reference loaded at Step 0. Operationalizes Wheeler's Phase 1 competitive audit + Johnson's investigate-phase competitive mapping.

## Why competitive audit matters

Positioning happens in a context. A brand cannot claim a position without knowing what competitors already occupy. A visual identity cannot find white space without mapping where competitors sit visually. A voice cannot differentiate without auditing what tones competitors use.

The competitive audit is not "knowing your competitors". It is producing the systematic map that brand-platform, brand-visual, brand-touchpoint, and brand-launch all read.

## Scope: defining the competitive set

### Three competitor types

**Direct competitors** (3-5):
- Same category
- Same audience
- Same price tier (or one tier adjacent)

**Indirect competitors** (2-4):
- Different category
- Same wallet/time share
- Audience overlaps significantly

**Aspirational reference brands** (1-2):
- Different stage (often larger or further along)
- Similar trajectory
- Brand sets a benchmark the brand might aspire to

### Selection discipline

The most common audit failure is competitive set inflation — including every brand the team is aware of. Discipline:

- Direct competitors must be brands the target customer actively considers
- Indirect competitors must compete for the same customer attention or budget
- Aspirational references must be relevant to the brand's category, not generic "great brands"

A focused competitive set of 6-10 brands produces better strategic clarity than a sprawling 20-brand inventory.

## Per-competitor audit framework

For each competitor, document:

### 1. Positioning claim
What does the competitor claim? Source: their website hero copy, their about page, their tagline, their primary advertising.

Construct in Ries-Trout format if not explicit:
> *For [their audience], [Competitor] is the [category] that [differentiator] because [reason to believe].*

### 2. Brand archetype (inferred)
Which Jung archetype best describes their personality? Look at:
- Tone of voice
- Visual identity
- Customer imagery
- Founder/leadership posture
- Brand stories told

### 3. Visual identity character

Document:
- **Logo character**: wordmark / logomark / emblem / monogram; style (modernist / heritage / craft / digital-native)
- **Color palette**: primary colors, supporting colors, overall temperature (warm / cool / neutral)
- **Typography**: primary typeface character (serif / sans / display / hand)
- **Photography style**: studio / documentary / lifestyle / abstract / iconic
- **Illustration use**: present / absent; style if present
- **Overall visual personality**: clean / textured / vintage / contemporary / classical / disruptive

### 4. Brand voice characteristics
- Tone: formal / casual / playful / authoritative / warm / clinical / oppositional
- Lexicon: jargon-heavy / plain-spoken / poetic / declarative / interrogative
- Sentence rhythm: long / short / mixed
- Use of humor: frequent / occasional / never

### 5. Customer persona served (inferred)
Demographic + psychographic synthesis from competitor's marketing.

### 6. Touchpoint footprint
Which touchpoints does the competitor invest in?
- Owned channels: website depth, content frequency, email program
- Earned channels: PR presence, press relationships
- Paid channels: advertising presence (where seen, what's said)
- Social: platforms used, posting cadence
- Retail: physical presence, distribution
- Events: trade shows, owned events, sponsorships

### 7. Recent narrative shifts
What has the competitor changed in the last 12-24 months? Tagline updates, positioning shifts, visual refreshes, expansion or contraction of touchpoints.

## White space identification

After per-competitor audits, synthesize the white space:

### Positioning white space
Plot competitors on a 2-axis matrix. Standard axis options:

**For consumer brands**: Price (mass to premium) × Style (classical to avant-garde)
**For B2B brands**: Scale (boutique to enterprise) × Approach (process-led to relationship-led)
**For luxury brands**: Heritage (new to established) × Conventionality (classical to disruptive)
**For technology brands**: Specialization (broad to vertical) × Customer scale (SMB to enterprise)

The unoccupied quadrants are positioning white space candidates.

### Visual white space
- Color territory: which colors and color combinations are unclaimed in the category
- Typographic territory: which type styles are unclaimed
- Logo type territory: if everyone uses wordmarks, an emblem or logomark may be white space
- Imagery territory: if everyone uses lifestyle photography, documentary or illustration may be white space
- Overall visual mood: if everyone is clean/modernist, textured/handmade may be white space

### Voice white space
- Tonal territory: if everyone is warm-and-friendly, considered-and-dry may be white space
- Lexical territory: if everyone uses jargon, plain-spoken may be white space
- Stance territory: if everyone is positive/affirming, oppositional/critical may be white space

### Audience white space
- Underserved segments within the category
- Demographic groups underrepresented in competitor targeting
- Psychographic segments competitors don't address

### Touchpoint white space
- Channels competitors underutilize
- Events/sponsorships unclaimed
- Content categories underserved

## Outputs

For each white space dimension, produce:
- **Description**: what the white space is
- **Defensibility**: why competitors haven't occupied it (deliberate avoidance? oversight? cost? regulatory?)
- **Strategic fit**: whether the brand's existing strengths position it to claim this space
- **Risk**: what occupying this space risks

White space that's truly unclaimed but also unstrategic for the brand is not opportunity — it's noise. The audit reports only white space that combines unoccupied + strategically fit.

## Bilingual discipline

Competitive audit citations preserve competitor brand names in original language. Audit synthesis written in the audit document's primary language. When the audit serves a TR-market brand, the audit includes TR competitive set even if international references inform the broader competitive context.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive set inflation | Audit covers 20+ brands; insight diluted | Narrow to 6-10 |
| Surface-level audit | Only logo + tagline noted per competitor | Audit all 7 dimensions per competitor |
| Confirmation bias | Audit confirms whatever the team already thought | Bring in outside reviewer |
| Aspirational-only references | All references are FAANG-tier brands | Include similar-stage competitors |
| Missing visual analysis | Competitive audit is verbal-only | Add visual character documentation |
| Stale audit | Audit references competitor materials >12 months old | Refresh; competitive landscapes shift |
