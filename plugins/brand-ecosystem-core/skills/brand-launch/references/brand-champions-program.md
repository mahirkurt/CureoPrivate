# Brand Champions Program Design

Reference for designing the brand champion network. Champions are the brand's distributed evangelists who continue the story-telling work after the launch event.

## Why champions matter

A brand strategy survives in real-world operations only when humans understand it well enough to apply it without checking a manual. The brand champion network is the institutional memory + adaptive interpretation layer that keeps the brand alive across thousands of daily decisions made by dozens or thousands of employees.

Without champions:
- Brand interpretation drifts (each team applies the brand in their own way)
- New employees inherit fragmentary understanding (lose the founding context)
- Customer-facing interactions vary widely
- Brand center / guidelines become museums (referenced rarely, applied poorly)

With champions:
- Consistent application emerges from internalized understanding
- New employees encounter the brand through peers, not just documents
- Customer interactions carry brand voice naturally
- Brand center / guidelines stay alive through champion-led education

## Champion selection criteria

### Role-based criteria
- **Customer-facing role**: sales, customer service, account management, partnerships, retail
- **Cross-functional liaison role**: project managers, business analysts, technical writers
- **Influencer role**: thought leaders within their function, conference speakers, internal authors

### Aptitude criteria
- **Storytelling**: comfortable explaining things in their own words, not reciting scripts
- **Empathy**: can read audience and adapt explanation
- **Brand affinity**: existing internal advocacy for the brand (not invented for the program)
- **Reliability**: shows up, follows through, can be counted on

### Sphere criteria
- **Internal reach**: connections across departments
- **External reach**: customer relationships, social media presence, professional community involvement
- **Departmental representation**: each major department has at least one champion

### Selection ratio
- 5-15% of employees typical
- Larger ratios dilute (everyone is a champion = no one is)
- Smaller ratios concentrate burden + risk single points of failure

For a 100-employee organization: 7-12 champions ideal.
For a 1,000-employee organization: 50-80 champions ideal.

## Champion role description

Champions take on:

### Responsibilities
- Tell the brand story internally when colleagues need orientation
- Tell the brand story externally in customer + partner conversations + content
- Apply brand thinking to ambiguous situations (where guidelines don't directly cover)
- Provide feedback to brand team where the brand resonates or breaks
- Help train new hires informally
- Contribute story content (customer stories, internal observations, behind-the-scenes)

### Recognition
- Champion badge / title addition to role description
- Public acknowledgment (all-hands, internal newsletter)
- Champion-only opportunities (early access to brand evolution thinking, brand team office hours)
- Optional: stipend or recognition bonus (rare but powerful)

### Time commitment
- Champion role is voluntary + role-augmenting, not full-time
- Expected: 2-4 hours/month sustained
- Concentrated periods around launch + refresh moments (10-20 hours during launch weeks)

## Champion program structure

### Initial training

Format options:
- **2-day intensive** (preferred for large cohorts): immersive, builds cohort identity, expensive
- **4 half-day workshops** (preferred for scattered cohorts): less disruption, more sustainable
- **Self-paced + cohort calls** (preferred for distributed teams): asynchronous + synchronous mix

Content arc:
- **Module 1: Brand foundation** — vision, mission, values, positioning (90 min)
- **Module 2: Brand story** — BrandScript deep dive, voice + tone, narrative arc (90 min)
- **Module 3: Visual + verbal system** — how to recognize on-brand vs off-brand work (60 min)
- **Module 4: Story crafting** — champion writes their own 60-second, 3-minute, 10-minute versions (180 min, longest module)
- **Module 5: Story testing + refinement** — champions tell stories to peers, receive feedback (120 min)
- **Module 6: Ongoing role + tools** — what champions do post-training, brand center walkthrough, support resources (60 min)

### Refresher cadence
- **Quarterly champion call** (60 min): brand updates, story refinement, peer learning
- **Annual champion summit** (1-day): deeper refresh, brand evolution preview, cohort renewal
- **Just-in-time refresh**: around major brand evolutions, product launches, market shifts

### Champion network connection
- **Dedicated channel** (Slack, Teams, internal social): champions-only space for cross-champion learning
- **Brand team office hours**: monthly drop-in for champion questions
- **Champion library**: curated content, examples, FAQ
- **Champion-only newsletter** (monthly): brand updates + champion spotlight

### Champion contribution mechanisms
- **Story submission portal**: champions submit customer stories, internal observations
- **Feedback channel**: champions report where the brand resonates or breaks
- **Peer training**: champions help train new hires (informal mentorship + formal pairing)
- **Content creation**: champions author thought leadership, social posts, conference talks (with brand team support)
- **Customer + partner introduction**: champions broker brand team access to specific accounts when needed

## Champion lifecycle

### Onboarding
- Selection notification (manager + employee both informed)
- Initial training (one of the formats above)
- Mentorship pairing with existing champion (if cohort 2+)
- Network access provisioned

### Active phase (months 1-18)
- Regular engagement with channel + calls
- Contribution to story collection + feedback
- Mentorship of newer champions
- Recognition + acknowledgment

### Sunset / transition (after 18-24 months)
Champions move on for various reasons:
- Role change (new role doesn't fit champion criteria)
- Burnout (program design failed to sustain energy)
- Departure (employee leaving company)
- Refresh (new champions joining; existing graduate to "alumni" status)

Sunset gracefully:
- Public recognition for service
- Optional alumni status (informal network, periodic invitations)
- Clear handoff of any ongoing responsibilities

## Common champion program failures

### Failure 1: Designation without development

Symptom: Champions named but no training program follows.

Result: Champions feel like a title rather than a role; they don't develop the storytelling capacity the role requires; the program signals brand team is checking a box.

Remedy: Treat champion designation as the start, not the end. The training is the program.

### Failure 2: Over-selection

Symptom: 30-50% of employees designated as champions.

Result: "Champion" becomes meaningless. Training quality degrades to lowest common denominator. Champions have no special status.

Remedy: Hold at 5-15%. Raise the bar. Make it an honor.

### Failure 3: Cop-model champions

Symptom: Champions positioned as enforcers (catching off-brand work, reporting non-compliance).

Result: Champions become unpopular internally; their colleagues avoid them; their effectiveness collapses.

Remedy: Champions are educators and helpers, not police. Reframe role.

### Failure 4: One-time training, no sustainment

Symptom: Initial training was excellent; quarterly refresh dropped after first cycle.

Result: Champion knowledge degrades over 12-18 months; champion enthusiasm wanes; program quietly dies.

Remedy: Sustained cadence is non-negotiable. Calendar the refreshes 18 months out from launch.

### Failure 5: Champion isolation

Symptom: Champions don't know other champions; no cross-pollination.

Result: Each champion operates alone; lessons aren't shared; brand team becomes single point of contact for all champion questions.

Remedy: Active network connection — channel, calls, summit. Champion-to-champion learning is the high-leverage activity.

### Failure 6: Brand team neglect

Symptom: Champion program runs without brand team investment; champions feel ignored.

Result: Champions disengage; program becomes hollow.

Remedy: Brand team holds office hours, attends quarterly calls, recognizes champion contributions publicly.

## Bilingual + multi-region champion programs

For multilingual / multi-region organizations:

- Champion training delivered in local language where possible
- Regional champion sub-networks (with translation/coordination infrastructure)
- Brand story adaptation to local cultural context — champions help calibrate
- Global champion summit (annual) brings sub-networks together

The brand maintains its core character; regional champions help it land authentically.

## Champion program success indicators

12 months post-launch, success indicators:

- Champion network retains 80%+ of designated champions
- Champion participation in calls remains > 60%
- Champion-contributed content appears regularly (stories, social, peer education)
- Brand team receives feedback from champion network monthly
- New hires report meeting champions in onboarding
- Customer feedback indicates consistent brand voice in customer-facing interactions
- Internal employee survey: 80%+ can articulate brand in their own voice (champions disproportionately responsible for this consistency)
