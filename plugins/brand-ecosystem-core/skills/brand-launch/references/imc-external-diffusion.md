# IMC External Diffusion — Rodriguez Doctrine Operationalized

Mandatory reference. Operationalizes Rodriguez's IMC Reimagined external diffusion (the public phase that follows internal saturation).

## When external diffusion begins

External diffusion begins ONLY when internal saturation indicators are met (see `phase5-doctrine.md`). Beginning earlier produces external visibility that internal employees can't support, which produces brand fragility.

If saturation indicators are partial, external diffusion can begin in a constrained form (soft launch, limited channels) while internal saturation completes. Full external launch waits for full saturation.

## External diffusion channel mix

### Owned channels (where the brand controls the message completely)

- **Website**: refreshed for launch; the canonical destination
- **Email**: existing customer base notification, prospect nurture, partner notification
- **Blog / content**: launch announcement, founder letter, behind-the-scenes content
- **Social media**: brand-owned accounts with launch content + sustained rollout

Owned channels lead the external launch. They establish the brand's narrative in its own voice before the message refracts through earned + paid channels.

### Earned channels (where the brand is mentioned by others)

- **PR / press**: journalist briefings, press release, exclusive launch coverage
- **Analyst / advisor briefings** (B2B): industry analyst notification, influencer briefing
- **Industry events**: conference panels, keynote opportunities, awards submissions
- **Customer + partner testimonials**: case studies, customer-authored content

Earned channels require lead time. PR cycles run 4-12 weeks for substantial coverage. Plan accordingly.

### Paid channels (where the brand pays for visibility)

- **Paid social** (Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, TikTok ads)
- **Search advertising** (Google, Bing)
- **Display + programmatic** (banner + native + connected TV)
- **Out-of-home** (billboard, transit, contextual print)
- **Print advertising** (trade publications, premium consumer media)
- **Podcast advertising / sponsorships**

Paid amplification follows earned + owned validation. Brands that launch with paid-first often discover their message isn't resonating, but they're already spending. Paid is for amplifying messages that have already proven they land.

### Event-based channels

- **Launch event**: in-person or virtual moment
- **Conference reveal**: timing launch with major industry event
- **Retail / pop-up moment**: physical brand experience
- **Customer + partner gathering**: invitation-only launch experience

Event-based moments concentrate attention and generate PR + content + advocacy assets.

## External launch sequencing

### Pre-launch teaser phase (optional, brand-dependent)

When appropriate:
- Identity-evolution rebrands (existing audience benefits from anticipation)
- Highly anticipated product launches (consumer beauty, gaming, automotive)
- Cultural moment-tied launches (conferences, season changes, anniversaries)

When inappropriate:
- B2B launches where teaser distracts from substance
- Sensitive timing (no teasers near major industry crisis)
- New brand with no audience to tease

Typical teaser content:
- Cryptic social posts (4-2 weeks pre-launch)
- "Something is coming" newsletter (2-1 week pre-launch)
- Cover-page logo reveal on website (1-3 days pre-launch)

### Launch peak

The day / week of public reveal. Components:

- **Website goes live** with new identity + content
- **Press release** distributed
- **All-hands moment** (internal, simultaneous with external)
- **Founder / CEO communication** (LinkedIn post, video message)
- **Customer notification** (email, in-app, app store update if applicable)
- **Social cascade**: brand accounts → leadership accounts → employee accounts → champion network
- **Paid campaigns activate** (only if pre-funded; not the same hour, but launch week)

The launch peak is intentionally concentrated. A diffuse "soft launch" without a defined peak moment dilutes attention.

### Post-launch sustainment (4-12 weeks)

The structured rollout following peak:

**Weeks 1-2**: amplification + responding to feedback
- Press follow-up
- Customer service responding to launch-related questions
- Social engagement with launch reactions
- Internal feedback collection (what worked, what surprised)

**Weeks 3-6**: deepening + secondary content
- Founder long-form content (essay, podcast appearances, conference talks)
- Customer story content
- Behind-the-scenes / making-of content
- Product / service deep-dives in brand voice

**Weeks 7-12**: integration into normal rhythm
- Brand becomes the consistent voice across all marketing
- New baseline established
- Lessons from launch fed into ongoing program

### Sustainment (3+ months onward)

Launch ends. Brand operations begin. The brand becomes the normal way the organization shows up.

## Story sequencing — which SB7 element leads externally

Drawing from `brand-story/references/sb7-framework-detailed.md`:

### Discovery launch (new brand, low awareness)
1. Lead: Character + Has-a-Problem (establish customer-centricity)
2. Support: Meets-a-Guide (introduce the brand)
3. Support: Plan + CTA

### Repositioning launch (existing brand, new positioning)
1. Lead: Philosophical problem (announce the stance)
2. Support: Meets-a-Guide (re-introduce the brand)
3. Support: Success transformation

### Category-creation launch (new category)
1. Lead: Philosophical problem (frame the gap)
2. Support: Has-a-Problem (validate audience experience)
3. Support: Meets-a-Guide + Plan (offer solution)

### Rebrand launch (existing brand, refreshed identity)
1. Lead: Continuity + evolution narrative (we are still us, but...)
2. Support: Why now (the strategic context)
3. Support: What's new (the identity shift)

The launch story sequence ≠ the BrandScript order. The BrandScript is canonical; the launch story sequence is performative.

## Customer migration (rebrand context)

When existing customers exist, migration discipline:

### Pre-launch customer communication
- Email to top-tier customers: personal note from senior leadership (1-2 weeks pre-launch)
- Email to general customer base: brand evolution notification (3-5 days pre-launch)
- Account-management touchpoint for enterprise / VIP customers (personal call, dedicated FAQ)

### Launch-day customer experience
- New visual identity at every customer touchpoint
- Customer support team briefed for questions
- FAQ document accessible
- Migration timeline for account portal / app interface updates

### Post-launch customer support
- 30-day enhanced response window
- Feedback collection
- Customer story collection (who likes the new identity, who has concerns)

### Asset migration timeline
- Same-day: website, email, social
- Week 1: customer portals, app interfaces, support documentation
- Weeks 1-4: physical asset migration (packaging in distribution, signage if multi-location)
- Quarter 1: full asset rotation (printed collateral runout, retail material refresh)

Customer migration done well preserves equity. Done badly damages trust.

## Multi-stakeholder launch (Corporate B2B context)

Corporate B2B launches affect:
- Customers (end users + buyers + influencers)
- Employees
- Investors (publicly traded or VC-backed)
- Partners (channel, technology, alliance)
- Regulators (in regulated sectors)
- Press / analysts
- Community

Each stakeholder receives differentiated communication on launch day:

| Stakeholder | Communication mode | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Employees | All-hands + email | Hour 0 |
| Customers (top-tier) | Personal notification | Hour 0-2 |
| Customers (general) | Email + in-app | Hour 0-24 |
| Investors | Disclosure / IR release | Hour 0 (regulatory timing) |
| Partners | Personal call + email | Hour 0-24 |
| Press | Embargo lifts | Hour 0 |
| Analysts | Briefing call | Hour 0 + Day 1 |
| Community | Public communication | Hour 0+ |

Coordinated multi-stakeholder launch requires substantial communication planning.

## Bilingual / multilingual launch

For multilingual brands:

- All launch assets prepared in primary languages of operation
- Bilingual customer communication
- Local PR in each language (not just translated; locally framed)
- Time-zone-aware launch sequencing (consider primary market work hours)
- Cultural adaptation of launch content (event format, holiday awareness, regional emphasis)

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Paid-first launch | Heavy advertising before owned + earned established | Lead with owned; paid amplifies validated message |
| Single channel launch | All eggs in one channel | Multi-channel orchestration |
| Customer surprise (rebrand) | Customers learn from social, not direct communication | Customer notification before public launch |
| Diffuse soft launch | No defined launch peak | Concentrate peak; sustain rollout |
| Post-launch abandonment | Launch peak then silence | Sustain 12 weeks structured rollout |
| Stakeholder oversight | One audience neglected in multi-stakeholder context | Map all stakeholders; differentiated comms |
| Press release ≠ launch | Launch = "we wrote a press release" | Press is one channel among many |
