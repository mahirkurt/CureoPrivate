# Wheeler Phase 5 Doctrine — Brand Launch & Governance

Mandatory reference. Synthesizes Wheeler's Phase 5 (Manage Assets) across the 6th + 5th editions.

## The thesis

A brand launch is not an event. It is a transition from a strategy + identity + touchpoint specification state into a living brand state where employees, customers, partners, and the world begin interacting with the brand on its terms.

Phase 5 has three operational concerns:
1. **Launch** — moving from internal preparation to external visibility
2. **Govern** — ensuring the brand is applied consistently as it grows
3. **Evolve** — managing the brand's adaptation to changing context

These three are interconnected. A launch that doesn't establish governance produces drift within months. Governance without launch saturation produces top-down enforcement of a brand employees don't yet understand. Evolution without governance produces fragmentation.

## Launch first principles

### Internal before external

The single most important Phase 5 principle. Rodriguez's IMC Reimagined doctrine and Wheeler's Phase 5 both converge on this. The external launch is the visible peak of a larger operation that began weeks or months earlier inside the organization.

A brand that hits the market with external campaigns while internal employees still ask "what's the new positioning?" has failed launch sequencing.

### The launch peak vs the launch arc

Common misconception: launch is a single day or week. Reality: launch is an arc:

- **Pre-launch (-8 to -1 weeks)**: internal saturation phase
- **Launch peak (week 0)**: external visibility starts
- **Post-launch (+1 to +12 weeks)**: structured external rollout
- **Sustainment (+3 months onward)**: integration into ongoing marketing rhythm

Brands that conceive launch as a single event under-resource the pre-launch and post-launch phases — exactly where launch success or failure is determined.

### Saturation indicators

When is internal launch sufficient to begin external launch? Saturation indicators:

- **Random employee survey**: 80%+ can articulate the new brand in their own voice
- **Manager messaging consistency**: senior managers using consistent brand language without coaching
- **Customer-facing team readiness**: sales, customer service, account managers can answer customer questions about the change
- **New hire absorption**: recent hires arriving with awareness of the brand story
- **Asset deployment readiness**: critical touchpoints production-ready (web, stationery, key environments)

Only when these indicators are met should external launch begin.

## Phase 5 Component: Brand Center

The digital infrastructure for ongoing brand application. Wheeler's term covers what other practitioners call "brand portal," "brand hub," "DAM" (digital asset management), or "brand intranet."

### Brand center functions

1. **Asset distribution** — single source of truth for logos, color, typography, photography, templates
2. **Guidelines reference** — search + browse the brand guidelines
3. **Approval workflow** — request asset adaptations, get approval, track changes
4. **Education** — onboarding, training modules, brand stories
5. **News + updates** — announcing brand evolution to internal + partner audiences

### Brand center governance models

Wheeler frames three governance models:

#### Cop model
- Centralized control
- All asset usage requires approval
- Locked guidelines
- Strong consistency, slow execution

Common in: large traditional brands, regulated sectors, brands recovering from inconsistency damage.

#### Hybrid model
- Centralized critical assets (logo, master color, primary typography)
- Self-serve secondary assets (templates, photography, social media)
- Tiered approval (high-stakes touchpoints reviewed; routine touchpoints self-managed)

Most common model today. Balances consistency with speed.

#### Concierge model
- Centralized enablement (excellent tools, training, support)
- Self-serve across most asset use
- Trust-based guidelines (clear principles, judgment-permitted application)
- High consistency through internalized understanding rather than enforcement

Aspirational for most brands. Requires substantial investment in training + tools. Best-suited to mature brand teams.

The launch plan declares which model is in effect post-launch (typically with an evolution path — start in cop, move to hybrid over 12 months, move to concierge over 24-36 months).

## Phase 5 Component: Brand Guidelines

The canonical document defining brand application. Wheeler's discipline:

### Brand guidelines vs brand book

Both terms used in practice; conventions vary:
- **Brand guidelines**: the operational reference — how to use the brand
- **Brand book**: the inspirational document — what the brand is and why

Some brands produce both. Most produce one document that integrates both concerns.

### Brand guidelines anatomy (Wheeler 6th ed.)

The document includes:
- Brand foundation (vision, mission, values, positioning)
- Verbal identity (name, voice, tone, examples)
- Visual identity (logo, color, typography, system)
- Application (per-touchpoint guidance)
- Governance (approval, request, update)

This anatomy is operationalized in `brand-guidelines-anatomy.md`.

### Format decisions

- **PDF only**: lowest investment, hardest to maintain, easy to share but easy to ignore
- **Web only**: best for search + browse + iterative update; requires platform investment
- **Web + PDF download**: most common; web is primary, PDF available for download
- **Interactive web platform**: highest investment; integrates downloads + tools + workflow

Most brands today produce web + PDF download. The PDF serves print + reference; the web serves discovery + workflow.

### Length discipline

A brand guidelines document longer than 80 pages is rarely read. A document shorter than 30 pages typically misses application detail.

The 40-60 page range is typical for substantial brand systems. The web version makes length less critical because users navigate to what they need.

## Phase 5 Component: Brand Champions / Storyteller Network

Operationalized in `brand-champions-program.md`.

### Champion role

- Internal: spreads the brand story across departments
- External: represents the brand authentically in customer + partner conversations
- Feedback: reports back to brand team where the brand resonates or breaks

### Champion ratio + selection

- Selection ratio: 5-15% of employees
- Selection criteria: customer-facing role + brand affinity + storytelling aptitude + sphere of influence
- Cross-department representation (not just marketing)

### Champion program structure

- Initial training: 2-day intensive or 4 half-day workshops
- Refresher cadence: quarterly
- Network connection: dedicated channel
- Recognition: badge, role addition, public acknowledgment
- Voluntary + role-based contribution

## Phase 5 Component: Onboarding Integration

Every new employee encounters the brand in onboarding. Wheeler discipline:

- Day 1: brand introduction (60-90 min)
- Week 1: brand immersion (deep dive, brand center walkthrough, champion meet)
- 30-day proficiency check (can the new hire articulate the brand?)
- 90-day contribution (new hire submits a brand observation or story)
- eLearning module: 15-30 min asynchronous, integrated into HR/LMS

A brand whose Day 1 onboarding doesn't include brand introduction is signaling that brand is non-essential to the role. This produces inconsistency at scale.

## Phase 5 Component: Brand KPI Dashboard

Wheeler emphasizes measurement. Without measurement, governance becomes opinion.

### Standard KPI categories

- **Awareness**: do target audiences know the brand exists?
- **Consideration**: do they consider it for purchase decisions?
- **Preference**: do they prefer it over alternatives?
- **Advocacy**: do they recommend it to others?

### Measurement cadence (Wheeler 6th ed.)

- Continuous: digital metrics (search, traffic, social)
- Monthly: sales / pipeline metrics, NPS samples
- Quarterly: structured brand tracking study (formal awareness/consideration/preference)
- Annual: comprehensive brand health assessment + competitive benchmarking

### Dashboard format

A brand KPI dashboard is a single-page (digital or print) summary updated monthly or quarterly. It travels to leadership reviews and informs brand investment decisions.

## Launch context variants

The Phase 5 doctrine adapts to launch context:

### Greenfield launch
- All Phase 5 components built from zero
- Maximum effort on awareness
- Champion program built alongside hiring

### Rebrand launch
- Existing customers + employees migrating
- Communication continuity primary
- Equity preservation in messaging
- Asset transition planning

### Sub-brand launch
- Parent brand context preserved
- Sub-brand architecture clarity
- Champion network may extend from parent

### M&A integration launch
- Two existing brands becoming one
- Dual-employee alignment most fragile
- Customer continuity critical
- Asset rationalization (what survives from each side)

### Refresh launch
- Visual evolution without strategic shift
- Continuity messaging
- Low-disruption rollout
- Champion network already exists; refresh their training

### Repositioning launch
- Strategic shift without identity replacement
- Narrative shift primary
- Expectation reset for existing customers
- Employee re-training on new positioning

The launch plan output adapts emphasis based on context.

## Wheeler 5th ed. additions (Cop to Concierge evolution)

The 5th edition explicitly documents the industry-wide evolution from Cop-model brand governance (top-down enforcement) toward Concierge-model (enabling self-serve excellence). This shift is driven by:

- Scale: brands now too large for centralized approval at every touchpoint
- Speed: digital touchpoint cadence exceeds traditional approval timelines
- Talent: distributed teams need autonomy to perform
- Tools: better tools (DAM, design systems) enable self-serve consistency
- Culture: knowledge workers reject Cop-model bureaucracy

The launch plan declares the brand's current model + target model + transition timeline.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Big bang launch | Single launch event without sustained rollout | Plan the arc, not just the peak |
| External-first | Public campaign before employees know the brand | Internal saturation before external |
| Guidelines as deliverable | 80-page PDF treated as launch endpoint | Treat guidelines as living tool |
| Brand center as DAM-only | Asset storage without education / workflow | Build full brand center, not asset dump |
| No measurement | Launch happens; results assumed | Build KPI dashboard from Day 1 |
| Cop forever | Mature brand still operating in cop model | Plan migration to hybrid or concierge |
| Champion program neglect | Champions designated but never trained or supported | Sustained program investment |
