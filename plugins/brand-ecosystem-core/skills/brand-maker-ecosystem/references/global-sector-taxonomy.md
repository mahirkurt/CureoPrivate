# Global Sector Taxonomy — Wiedemann / Taschen

Reference for situating brand names within established sector naming conventions. Derived from Julius Wiedemann's *Logo Design: Global Brands* (Taschen / Bibliotheca Universalis) — a curated taxonomy across 9 sectors with thousands of representative examples.

## The 9-sector taxonomy

| # | Sector | Common naming conventions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Industry & Trade | Founder names, geographic origin, descriptive compounds, sometimes Latin/Greek roots |
| 2 | Information & Media | Coined words, sometimes founder names, often short evocative monosyllables |
| 3 | Sports & Travel | Adventure-evoking, sometimes geographic, often kinetic phonetics |
| 4 | Health & Beauty | Latin/Greek roots, sometimes founder names (for premium), botanical references |
| 5 | Architecture & Construction | Founder names common, geographic references, sometimes material-evoking |
| 6 | Communications & Marketing | Coined words, sometimes acronyms, often modern phonetic strategies |
| 7 | Retail & Lifestyle | Wide range; from founder names (heritage) to coined consumer-tech (modern) |
| 8 | Services & Finance | Founder names, classical roots, sometimes Latin/Greek references for stability |
| 9 | Culture & Education | Founder names, place names, sometimes mission-referential phrases |

## Why sector taxonomy informs naming

When a brand enters a sector, it encounters established naming conventions:

- Customer expectations shaped by category exemplars
- Competitive set with sector-pattern names
- Industry-specific naming connotations
- Cultural/linguistic conventions within the sector

A new brand can:

- **Conform to sector convention** → easier customer parsing, harder differentiation
- **Diverge from sector convention** → harder customer parsing, easier differentiation
- **Selective divergence** → conform in one dimension (phonetic), diverge in another (semantic)

The strategic choice depends on positioning. A brand whose positioning is "considered alternative to mass-market" benefits from divergence. A brand whose positioning is "credible new entrant in established category" benefits from selective conformity.

## Sector-specific conventions (condensed)

### Industry & Trade
- B2B engineering, manufacturing, industrial services
- Names tend to be substantive, often founder-derived or geographic
- Recent trend: descriptive compounds (XYZ Systems, XYZ Manufacturing) yielding to coined words for differentiation
- Phonetic register: weighty, considered, sometimes classical

### Information & Media
- Publishing, broadcasting, digital media, journalism, content platforms
- Names tend to be short, memorable, sometimes invented
- Recent trend: increasingly verb-adjacent (allows usage in headlines, casual reference)
- Phonetic register: confident, often percussive

### Sports & Travel
- Athletic equipment, sports leagues, travel brands, hospitality, outdoor
- Names tend to be kinetic, sometimes geographic, often evocative
- Recent trend: experiential names over functional names
- Phonetic register: energetic, sometimes outdoor-evoking

### Health & Beauty
- Skincare, cosmetics, wellness, healthcare service (consumer-facing only), supplements (consumer)
- Names tend toward Latin/Greek roots for credibility, botanical references for natural positioning, founder names for premium
- Recent trend: science-credibility names + clean simple names
- Phonetic register: sensory, sometimes scientific, sometimes natural

### Architecture & Construction
- Architectural firms, real estate development, construction services
- Names tend to be founder-derived (especially partnerships: Smith & Jones)
- Recent trend: more abstract, more coined
- Phonetic register: substantive, often classical

### Communications & Marketing
- Agencies, marketing services, communications consultancies
- Names tend to be coined, sometimes invented, often distinctive in 1-2 syllables
- Recent trend: increasingly playful or oppositional
- Phonetic register: confident, modern, sometimes irreverent

### Retail & Lifestyle
- Apparel, accessories, home goods, lifestyle products, restaurants
- Range is wide: heritage founder names (Hermès, Tiffany) to modern coined (Glossier, Allbirds)
- Recent trend: founder names re-emerging in craft / heritage positioning
- Phonetic register: depends entirely on positioning

### Services & Finance
- Banking, insurance, asset management, professional services, consulting
- Names tend to be substantive, often founder-derived for trust, classical roots for stability
- Recent trend: fintech adopting more modern coined names; traditional finance staying classical
- Phonetic register: weighty, considered

### Culture & Education
- Museums, cultural institutions, schools, universities, training programs
- Names tend to be place names, founder names, mission-referential phrases
- Recent trend: more concept-driven names for new institutions
- Phonetic register: classical, substantive

## How brand-maker uses sector taxonomy

In Step 3 of brand-maker's 5-step methodology (Five-Category Brainstorm), the sector taxonomy informs:

- Category A (Descriptive): bias toward sector-conventional descriptors that work, avoid generic ones that don't
- Category B (Associative): draw from sector-specific associations
- Category C (Coined): consider sector tolerance for coined words
- Category D (Founder): consider sector tolerance for founder names
- Category E (Abstract): consider sector tolerance for abstract names

Then in Step 4 (Live Domain + Post-Digital Validation), check whether candidate names sit too close to existing sector exemplars (collision risk) or too far from sector conventions (parsing risk).

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Sector-blind naming | Name ignores sector convention; customer parsing fails |
| Sector-captive naming | Name conforms so closely it's indistinguishable from competitors |
| Wrong sector mapping | Brand named for one sector convention but operating in another (consumer brand named like B2B firm) |
| Outdated sector convention | Name follows sector convention from 20 years ago, sector has moved |

The Taschen reference provides a calibrating data set — when in doubt about a sector convention, the visible patterns across hundreds of mark examples reveal the convention.
