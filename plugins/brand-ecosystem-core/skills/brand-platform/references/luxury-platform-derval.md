# Luxury Platform — Opt-In Module (Derval Doctrine)

**Activation triggers**: "luxury", "premium", "super-luxury", "haute couture", "fine jewelry", "high-end", "exclusive", "Hermès", "Bottega Veneta", "Cartier", "Patek Philippe", "Loro Piana", "Aman", "Lalique", or similar reference brands.

When triggered, this module overlays additional structure on the platform document. Without trigger, this module is silent.

## Origin

Diana Derval's *Designing Luxury Brands: The Art and Science of Creating Game-Changers* (Springer 2024) — primary source for luxury-specific platform discipline. Derval's contribution synthesizes:
- Multi-sensory marketing research (Calvert, Pernod Ricard sensory campaigns)
- Hormonal Quotient® segmentation (proprietary, Derval Research)
- Color Vision research (Derval Color Test®)
- Wait Marketing doctrine
- Positioning map calibration for luxury tiers

## Module Outputs Added to Platform Document

When activated, the luxury module adds the following to the base eight-element platform:

### Addition 1 — Luxury Tier Positioning

The standard positioning statement (Element 6) is extended with explicit tier placement:

| Tier | Price multiplier vs mass-market | Distribution | Communication style |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Premium** | 2-5× | Selective retail, premium online | Aspirational but reachable |
| **Affordable luxury** | 5-10× | Department stores, brand stores | Status signaling accessible |
| **Luxury** | 10-30× | Brand boutiques, select multi-brand | Restrained, exclusive |
| **Super-luxury** | 30×+ | Brand-owned ateliers, by-appointment, invitation | Discreet, almost private |

The brand must claim exactly one tier. Drift across tiers dilutes brand codes.

**Decision rule**: A brand can succeed at premium *or* super-luxury — the question is consistency with target persona's wallet, distribution capacity, and communication discipline.

### Addition 2 — Brand Codes Specification

Brand codes are the signature elements that customers recognize before reading the brand name. For luxury brands, codes are non-negotiable continuity elements that signal authenticity.

**Required code categories**:

1. **Color code** — one or two non-negotiable colors that mean the brand (Hermès orange, Tiffany blue, Cartier red)
2. **Material code** — signature materials (Bottega Veneta intrecciato leather, Lalique crystal)
3. **Form code** — signature shapes or proportions (Birkin silhouette, Aston Martin grille)
4. **Pattern code** — if applicable (Burberry check, Goyard chevron)
5. **Sensory code** — beyond visual (Aman fragrance signature, Aston Martin engine note)

For new luxury brands, brand codes must be intentionally designed at the platform stage — not discovered post-launch. The luxury module produces a brand codes specification block.

### Addition 3 — Sensory Mix Primary Axis

Derval's research identifies five senses each carrying different weight depending on the luxury sub-category:

| Sense | Primary in categories | Operational implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Fashion, jewelry, watches, automotive | Logo, color, form, photography |
| **Tactile** | Leather goods, fashion, fine paper, materials | Materials, finish, weight, temperature |
| **Olfactory** | Fragrance, hospitality, retail environments | Scent strategy from atelier to packaging |
| **Auditory** | Automotive (engine note), hospitality (music), tech | Sonic identity, ambient sound design |
| **Gustatory** | Fine dining, wine, spirits, chocolate | Flavor architecture, tasting protocols |

The luxury platform must declare which sense leads. Multi-sense brands (hospitality especially) must name primary + secondary.

### Addition 4 — Hormonal Quotient® Persona Overlay

Derval's Hormonal Quotient® (HQ®) framework segments luxury shoppers by the influence of prenatal hormones (notably testosterone) on adult consumer behavior. The framework identifies that luxury purchase motivation varies systematically with HQ®:

- **High testosterone influence**: Drawn to status ornamentation, super-luxury, monogram-prominent items, social-ladder signaling
- **Low testosterone influence**: Drawn to discreet luxury, signature-quality items recognizable only to peers, anti-monogram

This overlay refines the persona constructed in Element 5. The luxury module adds an HQ® disposition descriptor to the persona block.

**Note**: Hormonal Quotient® is Derval Research proprietary terminology. Cite as such.

### Addition 5 — Wait Marketing Channel Disposition

Derval's Wait Marketing thesis: luxury customers are receptive to brand engagement at specific moments of contextual openness — airport lounges, hotel concierge moments, private banking sessions, atelier appointments, in-flight luxury catalogs.

The luxury module produces a channel disposition declaration:

| Channel | Brand presence stance |
|---|---|
| Airport (departures, lounges) | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Travel retail (duty-free, in-flight) | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Hotel (concierge, in-room amenity) | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Private banking / wealth management | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Member's clubs / private societies | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Atelier / by-appointment | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Fashion week / industry events | Primary / Secondary / Absent |
| Auction houses | Primary / Secondary / Absent |

A super-luxury brand typically claims 2–3 channels as Primary, the rest as Secondary or Absent. Spreading across all channels dilutes super-luxury positioning.

### Addition 6 — Positioning Map Calibration

The luxury module produces an explicit positioning map (text-described, not graphic). Two axes:

- X-axis: Price (mass to super-luxury)
- Y-axis: Style (classical to avant-garde)

The brand plots itself + 5–8 competitors. The brand's specific quadrant placement is documented with justification.

**Sample placements (synthesized, not literal)**:
- Hermès: super-luxury / classical
- Bottega Veneta: luxury / contemporary
- Off-White: affordable luxury / avant-garde
- Patek Philippe: super-luxury / classical
- Hublot: luxury / avant-garde

## How the Luxury Module Affects Downstream Skills

When this module is active, downstream skills receive different parameters:

- **brand-story**: Hero (the customer) is wealthier, more discriminating, more privacy-seeking; villain is "obvious display"; narrative arc emphasizes quiet recognition over loud success
- **brand-maker**: Name candidates lean toward founder names, heritage references, classical forms; avoid contemporary tech-style coinages unless the brand is positioned as avant-garde luxury
- **brand-visual**: Color palette includes neutral classical foundations; typography leans serif or classical sans; favicons remain restrained; print production specifications dominate over digital
- **brand-touchpoint**: Packaging is primary; retail environment becomes the most important touchpoint; digital touchpoints are restrained; in-person service touchpoints get elevated treatment
- **brand-launch**: Internal-first diffusion is even more critical (luxury brands cannot afford internal misalignment); external launch is event-based rather than campaign-based; PR-led rather than advertising-led

## Bilingual Considerations for Luxury Brands

Luxury brands operating in Türkiye must navigate:
- French/Italian heritage signaling vs Turkish market relevance
- Arabic Gulf market presence (high luxury concentration)
- EN-TR-FR-AR multilingual touchpoint communications

The luxury module's positioning statement EN+TR should also produce FR draft if European heritage is a brand code element, and AR draft if Gulf distribution is a Primary channel.

## What This Module Does NOT Cover

- Conjoint analysis of luxury attribute trade-offs (commission externally)
- Hormonal Quotient® testing of specific customer samples (Derval Research consultation required)
- Pricing strategy across tiers (separate pricing study required)
- Counterfeit/IP protection strategy (legal counsel required)
- Wholesale vs retail distribution model design (operational strategy work)

The module produces strategic positioning for luxury. Tactical execution remains downstream.
