# Persona Construction — Demographic + Psychographic + Maslow

This reference operationalizes Element 5 (Target Persona) of the platform document. Loaded by default for every brief.

## Why persona construction is mission-critical

Every downstream skill depends on knowing precisely who the brand serves. A vague persona produces vague positioning, vague names, vague visual identity, and vague launch. Conversely, a sharp persona — one a copywriter could draw from a single page — radiates clarity through the entire system.

The discipline is to construct the persona from three coordinated layers: demographic, psychographic, and Maslow. Each layer answers a different question; combined, they produce a customer the brand can address.

## Layer 1 — Demographic Block

Statistical attributes. Necessary but insufficient. Demographics tell you who the person is in census categories; they do not tell you how the person thinks.

### Required fields

- **Age range** — typically a 10–15 year band, narrower for niche brands
- **Gender mix** — only specify if salient to the brand (avoid gendering by default)
- **Income band** — relevant for pricing strategy
- **Geography** — region + density (urban/suburban/rural)
- **Education** — completed level
- **Occupation cluster** — broad category, not specific job titles
- **Household composition** — single/couple/family, dependents
- **Major life stage** — if relevant (early career / parenting / empty nest / retirement)

### What demographics cannot do

Demographics cannot tell you:
- What the person believes
- What media they consume
- Why they buy in this category
- What unmet needs they have
- How they describe the problem the brand solves

For these, you need psychographics.

## Layer 2 — Psychographic Profile (Slade-Brooking framework)

Psychographics describe values, attitudes, interests, lifestyles. They tell you not who the person is, but how the person thinks.

### Young & Rubicam's 4Cs Segmentation

A globally validated psychographic segmentation system, derived from Maslow's hierarchy and applied across 50+ countries by Young & Rubicam.

| Segment | % of population | Description | Brand affinity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Resigned** | 10% | Older, brand-loyal, value-oriented, prefers familiar. Centered on safety, economy, familiarity, expert opinion. | Brands offering nostalgia, simple messages, low risk |
| **Struggler** | 8% | Younger, materially-limited, sensation-seeking, often alienated from mainstream. | Brands offering sensation and escape, visual impact |
| **Mainstreamer** | 30% | Conventional, family-focused, risk-averse, everyday domesticity. | Well-known, value-oriented brands; emotionally warm, secure, reassuring |
| **Aspirer** | 13% | Younger, status-seeking, materialistic, image-driven. | Trendy, fun, unique brands; status-conferring |
| **Succeeder** | 16% | Confident, organized, goal-oriented, premium-affinity, leadership roles. | Brands offering prestige and reward; pampering; evidence-based |
| **Explorer** | 9% | Discovery-driven, early-adopter, novelty-seeking, young at heart. | Brands offering new sensations, indulgence, instant effects |
| **Reformer** | 14% | Intellectually-driven, socially-conscious, principles-led, anti-status. | Brands offering authenticity, harmony, concepts |

### How to use 4Cs in persona construction

1. **Pick the primary segment** the brand serves
2. **Identify the secondary segment** the brand also touches (most brands serve 1.5 segments well)
3. **Avoid claiming to serve more than 2 segments** — brands that try to be everything to everyone are nothing to anyone

### Writing the psychographic paragraph

After segment assignment, write a 150–250 word paragraph describing the persona. The paragraph must cover:

- **Values** — what they believe matters
- **Attitudes** — how they evaluate the world
- **A typical day** — concrete daily routine
- **Media habits** — what they read, watch, listen to
- **Brand affinities** — three brands they love in adjacent categories
- **Unmet needs** — what current category brands fail to give them

### Example psychographic paragraph (synthesized)

> Daniel is a thirty-six-year-old independent design consultant working from a converted loft in central Istanbul. He left a top-tier agency three years ago to build a practice on his own terms. He treats his daily coffee ritual as the small architecture that anchors a working day otherwise full of context-switching — meetings, client calls, deep work sessions. He drinks specialty roasters' beans, brews on a Hario V60, follows roaster Instagram accounts more closely than design Instagram accounts. He values craft, transparency about provenance, and small businesses that know what they're doing. He reads the Monocle weekly newsletter, listens to design podcasts at the gym, and increasingly skeptical of glossy lifestyle brands that he sees as performing rather than practicing their values. What current coffee brands fail to give him is honest communication: most either oversell their "specialty" credentials or hide behind generic premium imagery. He wants to know who roasted the beans, when, and why the roaster chose them.

This paragraph is sufficient material for downstream skills.

## Layer 3 — Maslow Layer Placement

Where on Maslow's hierarchy does this brand operate primarily?

| Layer | Description | Emotional register implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Physiological** | Food, water, shelter, sleep, basic survival | Practical, evidence-led, no metaphor — the customer is making survival decisions |
| **Safety** | Security, employment, resources, health, property | Trust signaling, risk reduction, reliability emphasis |
| **Love & Belonging** | Friendship, family, intimacy, sense of connection | Warmth, community, shared experience |
| **Esteem** | Respect, status, recognition, freedom, achievement | Confidence, achievement signaling, peer recognition |
| **Self-Actualization** | Creativity, purpose, growth, transcendence | Aspiration, meaning, transformation |

### How Maslow placement affects downstream

A brand operating at **Safety** writes very different copy from a brand operating at **Self-Actualization**:

- *Safety* register: clear, reassuring, evidence-rich, predictable
- *Self-Actualization* register: aspirational, meaningful, transformative, possibility-rich

Mistakes here cascade: a brand operating at Safety written in Self-Actualization register sounds bombastic. A brand operating at Self-Actualization written in Safety register sounds timid.

### Multi-level brands

Some brands operate at multiple Maslow levels for different customer segments. A health insurance brand may sell Safety to one segment and Self-Actualization to another. In these cases, name the primary level and acknowledge the secondary; do not try to communicate to both registers in the same campaign.

## Putting the three layers together

A complete persona is the synthesis of demographic + psychographic + Maslow. Here is the integration discipline:

1. **Demographic places** the persona in census categories
2. **Psychographic explains** how the persona thinks
3. **Maslow positions** the persona's primary needs

When the three align coherently, downstream skills have everything they need. When they conflict (e.g., demographic placement contradicts psychographic segment), the persona is incoherent — typically because the brand is trying to serve two distinct audiences and refusing to choose.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Demographics-only persona | Persona is statistical; no human emerges | Add psychographic paragraph |
| Aspirational persona | Persona describes who the brand wishes its customers were, not who they actually are | Validate against real customer data |
| Mass-market over-targeting | "Persona" is "anyone with money" | Pick a primary 4Cs segment |
| Persona-as-stereotype | Persona reduces a group to a caricature | Refine with specific behaviors over identity labels |
| Persona-as-everything | Persona claims to serve all 4Cs segments | Choose primary + secondary, exclude others |

## Multi-persona brands

Some brands legitimately serve multiple personas (e.g., B2B brands serving end-users, buyers, and influencers; consumer brands serving primary buyer and primary user when distinct).

Discipline:
- Construct each persona separately, with full demographic + psychographic + Maslow blocks
- Name each persona explicitly (typically by role: "the buyer", "the user", "the influencer")
- Define the relationship between personas (who decides, who uses, who influences)
- Downstream skills receive all persona blocks and adapt accordingly

Most brands have one dominant persona and at most two secondary personas. More than three personas typically indicates targeting confusion.
