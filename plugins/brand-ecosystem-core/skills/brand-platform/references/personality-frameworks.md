# Personality Frameworks — Three-Layer Composite Reference

This reference operationalizes the three-layer brand personality system used in Element 4 of the platform document. Loaded on demand (every brief by default).

## Layer A — Jung Archetypes (Pearson & Mark)

The 12 archetypes derive from Carl Jung's collective unconscious framework, adapted to brand strategy by Margaret Mark and Carol Pearson in *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001).

### Selection criteria

Pick the archetype that best describes the brand's relationship with the world — its primary motivational driver, its enemy, its promise, and the human aspiration it serves.

### The 12 Archetypes — Full Operational Matrix

| Archetype | Driver | Strategy | Enemy | Verbal register | Visual register | Typical sectors | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sage** | Truth | Use intelligence to understand the world | Ignorance | Knowledgeable, measured, citation-rich | Reduced, classical, restrained | Education, publishing, research, consulting | Becomes dry; loses warmth |
| **Outlaw** | Liberation | Disrupt or destroy | Powerlessness | Defiant, raw, anti-establishment | High contrast, broken grids, unconventional | Streetwear, anti-establishment media, disruptive tech | Becomes performative; loses edge |
| **Creator** | Self-expression | Create something of enduring value | Mediocrity | Artistic, ambitious, craft-focused | Maker-driven, material-aware, process-visible | Design, craft, creative software | Becomes self-indulgent; loses customer focus |
| **Hero** | Mastery | Prove worth through courageous action | Weakness | Bold, brave, action-oriented | Strong, athletic, victorious | Sports, military, leadership development | Becomes arrogant; appears overcompensating |
| **Caregiver** | Service | Care for and protect others | Selfishness | Warm, compassionate, attentive | Soft, embracing, nurturing | Healthcare, hospitality, childcare, NGO | Becomes saccharine; loses competence signaling |
| **Magician** | Power | Transform reality through vision | Stagnation | Transformative, visionary, oracular | Cinematic, surreal, transcendent | Wellness, transformative tech, esoteric services | Becomes vague; appears charlatan |
| **Everyman** | Belonging | Connect with others | Exclusion | Approachable, fair, common-sense | Familiar, accessible, unintimidating | Mass-market consumer, community brands | Becomes generic; loses memorability |
| **Lover** | Intimacy | Be desired and desirable | Loneliness | Sensual, devoted, emotional | Lush, tactile, warm | Beauty, fashion, romance, fine dining | Becomes superficial; trapped in surface |
| **Jester** | Joy | Have fun, lighten the mood | Boredom | Playful, irreverent, witty | Bright, kinetic, unexpected | Entertainment, snacks, social-first brands | Becomes lightweight; not taken seriously |
| **Ruler** | Control | Establish order and authority | Chaos | Authoritative, refined, definitive | Heritage, symmetrical, ornamented | Luxury, finance, premium automotive, prestige | Becomes pompous; appears out of touch |
| **Innocent** | Safety | Maintain optimism and purity | Corruption | Optimistic, pure, hopeful | Clean, white, naive | Wellness, baby products, faith-based brands | Becomes naive; appears unrealistic |
| **Explorer** | Freedom | Discover and experience | Conformity | Adventurous, restless, curious | Open, expansive, journey-imaged | Outdoor, travel, adventure technology | Becomes rootless; appears unreliable |

### Primary + Secondary archetypes

Most strong brands carry a primary archetype with a secondary that modulates it. The secondary archetype refines the primary; it doesn't compete with it.

Examples (synthesized for illustration, not literal):
- *Magician + Sage* — transformation grounded in deep knowledge (high-end wellness, transformative consulting)
- *Hero + Caregiver* — brave service in defense of others (frontline healthcare, public safety)
- *Outlaw + Creator* — rebellion through making (independent fashion, anti-establishment design)
- *Innocent + Lover* — pure devotion (heritage romance, ethical luxury)
- *Ruler + Magician* — authoritative transformation (legacy private banking, prestige consulting)

## Layer B — Aaker 5-Dimension Scoring

Jennifer Aaker's 1997 research identified five reliable dimensions of brand personality:

1. **Sincerity** — down-to-earth, honest, wholesome, cheerful
2. **Excitement** — daring, spirited, imaginative, up-to-date
3. **Competence** — reliable, intelligent, successful
4. **Sophistication** — upper-class, charming
5. **Ruggedness** — outdoorsy, tough

Each brand scores 0–10 on each dimension. The result is a polygon — the brand's personality fingerprint.

### Why both archetype AND Aaker?

Archetypes give qualitative identity. Aaker gives quantitative differentiation. Two brands sharing the *Magician* archetype can differ sharply:
- A high-end wellness brand: Sophistication 9, Competence 7, Excitement 6, Sincerity 5, Ruggedness 1
- An outdoor adventure tech brand: Excitement 9, Ruggedness 8, Competence 7, Sophistication 3, Sincerity 5

The Aaker profile catches what archetype alone misses.

### Calibration discipline

When scoring, anchor against three reference brands you know well in the same category. If your scoring places your brand higher on every dimension than every reference, the scoring is uncritical — recalibrate.

## Layer C — Voice Traits

Three to five concrete adjectives describing the brand's verbal register. The discipline:

1. **Each adjective adds new dimension** — no synonyms.
2. **Adjectives are not aspirational** — they describe how the brand actually sounds in operations.
3. **Adjectives are testable** — a copywriter must be able to recognize compliance.

### Common failure modes

| Failure | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Synonymous traits | "Friendly, warm, approachable" | Pick one + add diversity: "Friendly, candid, generous" |
| Empty traits | "Premium, quality, excellence" | Replace with operational adjectives |
| Contradictory traits | "Playful and authoritative" | Resolve: "Authoritative with occasional dry humor" |
| Untestable traits | "Sincere" | Operationalize: "Plainspoken — never euphemizes problems" |

### Voice principles vs voice traits

Layer C voice **traits** are adjectives (3–5).
Element 8 voice **principles** are directives with examples (3–5).
They are related but distinct. Traits describe the personality; principles instruct the operations.

## Composite Output for Downstream Skills

The three layers combine into a personality dossier downstream skills consume:

```yaml
brand_personality:
  jung_primary: Sage
  jung_secondary: Magician
  aaker_5d:
    sincerity: 6
    excitement: 4
    competence: 9
    sophistication: 8
    ruggedness: 1
  voice_traits: [erudite, plainspoken, generous, dry]
```

This dossier is sufficient for:
- brand-story to set the narrator's voice
- brand-maker to bias name candidates toward this personality (e.g., Sage+Magician favors substantive coined names over playful ones)
- brand-visual to constrain color/typography/form choices
- brand-touchpoint to set tone of customer-facing copy
- brand-launch to design the brand champion's persona
