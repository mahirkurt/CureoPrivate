# IMC Reimagined Plan — Diffusion Protocol

Reference loaded when narrative coverage extends beyond the BrandScript itself. Operationalizes Miri Rodriguez's *Brand Storytelling* (2020/2024 ed.) Integrated Marketing Communications Reimagined doctrine.

## The thesis

Traditional advertising-led launches push brand stories outward through paid media before internal alignment exists. Result: external customers see a story that internal employees can't tell consistently. Brand experience fractures.

IMC Reimagined inverts the sequence: **brand stories must be socialized internally before they go to market externally.** Employees become the brand's first storytellers. External diffusion follows internal saturation.

This is now standard practice for high-performing brands. It is still rare practice for the median brand.

## The two-phase architecture

### Phase 1: Internal-First Diffusion

The brand story moves through the organization in deliberate sequence:

1. **Leadership briefing** — executives + senior managers internalize the BrandScript
2. **Employee evangelization** — all employees encounter the BrandScript through structured engagement
3. **Champion identification** — internal storytellers are identified by role and aptitude
4. **Storyteller training** — champions learn to tell the brand story in their own authentic voice
5. **Internal-facing artifacts** — employee handbook, onboarding, all-hands content created or revised

### Phase 2: External Diffusion

Only after Phase 1 has saturated:

1. **Channel selection** — where the story will first appear externally
2. **Story sequencing** — which SB7 element leads, which support
3. **Channel mix** — PR, content, social, advertising in deliberate proportion
4. **Story testing** — protocols for validating the story is landing
5. **Refinement loop** — how feedback re-enters Phase 1

## Phase 1 — Internal Diffusion in Detail

### Leadership briefing

**Goal**: Senior leadership can articulate the BrandScript in their own voice within 30 minutes of preparation.

**Format options**:
- Leadership retreat with the BrandScript as primary artifact (1-2 days)
- Series of structured leadership team workshops (4-6 sessions of 90 min)
- One-on-one leadership briefings with the brand strategist (used when leadership team is fragmented)

**Critical**: Leadership must not just *receive* the BrandScript. They must *engage* with it — challenge it, ask questions, find their own voice in it. A BrandScript imposed top-down without dialogue rarely survives.

### Employee evangelization

**Goal**: Every employee can tell the brand story in their own words, in 60 seconds, when asked at a dinner party.

**Mechanisms**:
- All-hands presentation introducing the BrandScript (60-90 min)
- Department workshops adapting the BrandScript to each department's reality (60-90 min per department)
- Story circles — small-group sessions where employees rehearse telling the brand story
- Storytelling field cards — pocket-sized references with the seven SB7 elements
- New-hire onboarding integration — BrandScript becomes a Day-1 conversation

**Time scale**: For organizations >100 employees, expect 3-6 months for saturation. For organizations <50 employees, 4-8 weeks.

### Champion identification

Not every employee becomes a champion. Champions are identified by:

- **Role**: customer-facing roles (sales, customer service, account management)
- **Aptitude**: natural storytellers, employees who already articulate the brand well
- **Authenticity**: employees whose personal voice aligns with the brand voice
- **Reach**: employees with internal or external audience (social media presence, community involvement)

Selection ratio: typically 5-15% of employees become formal champions. Larger ratios dilute the program.

### Storyteller training

**Goal**: Champions tell the brand story authentically — in their own voice — without losing fidelity to the BrandScript.

**Content**:
- BrandScript deep dive (1 session, 90 min)
- Story crafting workshop (2 sessions, 90 min each) — champions write their own version
- Story testing (1 session, 90 min) — champions tell their stories to peers, receive feedback
- Refresher cadence (quarterly, 60 min)

**Outcome artifact**: Each champion has a 60-second, 3-minute, and 10-minute version of the brand story in their own voice.

### Internal-facing artifacts

Created or revised after BrandScript approval:

- **Employee handbook**: brand story integrated as opening section
- **Onboarding curriculum**: Day-1 BrandScript module
- **All-hands presentations**: BrandScript becomes recurring reference
- **Internal newsletter**: monthly story spotlight (an employee, a customer, a project)
- **Slack/internal social**: dedicated story channel
- **Manager toolkit**: scripts for telling the brand story in team contexts

## Phase 2 — External Diffusion in Detail

### When to begin Phase 2

Phase 1 saturation indicators:
- Random employee survey: 80%+ can articulate the BrandScript in their own voice
- Leadership messaging: consistent across team meetings without coaching
- Customer-facing teams: independently using BrandScript language with customers
- New hires: arriving with awareness of the brand story

Begin Phase 2 only when these indicators are met. Premature Phase 2 launch produces inconsistency.

### Channel selection

Where will the story first appear externally? Selection criteria:

- **Affinity**: channels the target persona inhabits
- **Control**: channels where the brand can sustain narrative coherence
- **Earned vs paid balance**: earned media (PR, organic content) before paid (advertising)
- **Scale match**: channel scale appropriate to brand stage

Lead channel typically owned media (website, content, email) — where the brand controls the narrative completely. Paid amplification follows once organic resonance is confirmed.

### Story sequencing

Which SB7 element leads externally? Different sequences for different launch contexts:

**Discovery launch** (new brand, low awareness):
1. Lead: Character + Has-a-Problem (establish customer-centricity)
2. Support: Meets-a-Guide (introduce the brand)
3. Support: Plan + CTA (give the audience a way forward)

**Repositioning launch** (existing brand, new positioning):
1. Lead: Philosophical problem (announce the stance)
2. Support: Meets-a-Guide (re-introduce the brand)
3. Support: Success (paint the new transformation)

**Category-creation launch** (new category, audience needs education):
1. Lead: Philosophical problem (frame the category gap)
2. Support: Has-a-Problem (validate audience's experience)
3. Support: Meets-a-Guide + Plan (offer the solution)

### Channel mix

Typical Phase 2 channel mix (for first 6 months):

| Channel | Role | % effort |
|---|---|---|
| Earned PR | Story establishment | 25-35% |
| Owned content (website, blog, video) | Story deepening | 25-30% |
| Organic social | Story amplification | 15-20% |
| Email | Story nurture | 10-15% |
| Paid advertising | Story scale (only after earned validation) | 10-20% |

Brand vertical and budget affect ratios significantly. Luxury brands tilt toward PR + event. B2B brands tilt toward content + relationship-based. DTC tilts toward owned content + paid social.

### Story testing protocol

How the brand validates the story is landing:

**Quantitative signals**:
- Unaided brand recall in target segment
- Aided message recall (do customers play back the story elements?)
- Inbound qualified lead language (do prospects use the brand's language?)

**Qualitative signals**:
- Customer interviews (do they describe the brand in BrandScript terms?)
- Customer-service language audit (do CS interactions use brand voice?)
- Sales conversation audit (do sales conversations follow BrandScript flow?)

**Cadence**: Quarterly review for first year, semi-annual thereafter.

### Refinement loop

Story feedback flows back into Phase 1:

- Champion network reports anomalies (story not landing in certain contexts)
- Customer feedback identifies misalignment (what customers expect vs what they receive)
- Sales/CS data flags weak narrative spots
- These feed quarterly Phase 1 refinement: revised champion training, updated artifacts, BrandScript adjustment if needed

## Pre-launch / Pre-employee Brand Variant

For solopreneur or pre-launch brands without employees:

**Modified Phase 1**:
- Founder + co-founder briefing (replaces leadership)
- First five hires onboarded with BrandScript as primary brand introduction
- Founder builds their own 60-second / 3-minute / 10-minute versions
- External advisors / mentors briefed (these become external champions)

**Modified Phase 2**:
- Founder is primary external storyteller for first 12 months
- Founder-led PR (interviews, podcasts, conference talks)
- Founder-led content (newsletter, social, video)
- Champion network is grown alongside revenue

This modification preserves the IMC Reimagined principle while adapting to the smaller scale.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising-first launch | External campaign launches before employees know the story | Pause campaign, run Phase 1 first |
| Top-down imposition | Leadership announces BrandScript without dialogue | Re-run with structured engagement |
| Champion overproduction | 50%+ of employees designated as champions | Reduce to 5-15%; raise the bar |
| Storyteller scripting | Champions reciting memorized scripts | Re-train; emphasize authentic voice |
| Skipped refinement loop | No mechanism to bring feedback back to Phase 1 | Establish quarterly review |
| Premature Phase 2 | External launch before internal saturation | Wait for saturation indicators |
