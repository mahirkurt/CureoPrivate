## 2. Enterprise Stationery System

B2B stationery is not a creative exercise; it is an **operational system** spanning role variants, multi-location offices, multi-language adaptations, and integration with enterprise IT (email signatures, document templates, presentation systems).

### 2.1 Role-Based Business Card Variants

Specify card variants for typical roles:
- Executive (C-suite): often heavier stock, embossed title, sometimes letterpress; minimum two language variants for global executives
- Director / VP: standard executive stock; clear title hierarchy
- Manager / Individual Contributor: standard stock; functional layout
- Sales: includes mobile number, sometimes QR for vCard
- Operations / Field: durable lamination or rounded corners

Each variant follows the same identity system; differentiation is restrained — never imply hierarchy through ornament, only through subtle stock/finish.

### 2.2 Letterhead System

| Variant | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Corporate letterhead | Primary identity, full address block, registration details, multiple language footer if applicable |
| Executive letterhead | Personalized version with role/name in header area |
| Legal/Contracts letterhead | Often distinct subtle variant — heavier stock, more formal lockup, full legal entity name |
| Press release letterhead | Variant with media contact in footer; sometimes hosted PR identity for the comms team |
| Notice/Memo internal | Sometimes a stripped-down internal-only variant, less elaborate |

### 2.3 Envelopes

Multiple envelope sizes specified with corresponding letterhead pairings:
- DL / #10 — standard business
- C5 / A2 — quarterly reports, ceremony
- C4 / 9x12 — proposals, contracts
- Padded / Tyvek — for shipping documents
- Internal interoffice — distinctive marking, often distinct stock

Specify: stock, color, position of return address, position of recipient address, window or non-window, gum or peel-and-seal closure.

### 2.4 Digital Stationery — Email Signatures

The most-used corporate touchpoint, often most neglected. Specify:
- Layout (image-only, text-only, hybrid)
- Hierarchy (name, title, company, contact, mobile, address, social)
- Visual element (logo, color band, photo for executives)
- Pronunciation (name + role + tagline + legal disclaimer if regulated)
- Variants: standard, sales, legal, executive
- Technical: HTML signature with fallback text, image hosting, mobile rendering
- Update mechanism: centrally managed via tool (Exclaimer, CodeTwo, etc.) or distributed template

### 2.5 Document Template System

Enterprise brands ship internal document templates:
- Word / Google Docs letter templates (per letterhead variant)
- Word / Google Docs memo templates
- Word / Google Docs report templates
- Excel / Google Sheets templates with cover sheet, table styles, chart styles
- Email templates (Outlook signatures, distribution list templates)

These templates are governance artifacts — they ensure brand expression in unbranded daily work output (every spreadsheet, every memo, every internal email).

## 3. Corporate Signage and Wayfinding

### 3.1 Building Identity

| Touchpoint | Mass Retail | Enterprise Specification |
|-----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Lobby identity | Brand mark prominent | Brand mark + sub-brand or division if multi-brand architecture; typeface choice often more formal |
| Reception desk | Branded panel | Sometimes corporate art commission; brand mark integrated into architecture |
| Exterior signage | Storefront-style | Building-mounted; sometimes hidden from street unless flagship |
| Address numerals | Standard signage | Often architectural — cast metal or stone-engraved |

### 3.2 Interior Wayfinding

Enterprise environments require systematic wayfinding:
- Floor identifiers
- Department/team area markers
- Meeting room identifiers (often with booking system integration)
- Restroom and amenity signage
- Emergency / safety signage (regulated; must conform to ISO 7010, OSHA, or local equivalents)
- Conference and event-specific wayfinding (often temporary printed signage)

Specify: substrate (often cast acrylic, brushed aluminum, or hand-finished wood), typography hierarchy, color use, height position, ADA / accessibility conformance.

### 3.3 Meeting Room Identity

Enterprise meeting rooms often have themed identity systems:
- Numbered/lettered (functional)
- Geographic (cities, mountains, rivers)
- Conceptual (values, virtues, periodic elements)
- Honoring (employee names, customer names, historical figures)

The choice signals corporate culture. Each room's identity then appears in calendar systems, on door plaques, in room booking software.

## 4. Conference and Trade Show Architecture

Trade shows are a **major B2B touchpoint** with specific architectural requirements.

### 4.1 Booth Specifications

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Footprint | 10x10, 10x20, 20x20, 20x30, custom — based on tier of show |
| Construction | Modular system (Octanorm, Beamatic) for cost; custom-built for flagship shows |
| Identity surfaces | Backwall, header, kiosk panels, monitor frames |
| Demo stations | Number of stations, technology infrastructure, demo script integration |
| Meeting space | Sit-down areas, sometimes private meeting rooms within booth |
| Storage | Lockable closet for collateral, demo equipment, marketing swag |
| Counter / bar | Sometimes coffee station or wine bar in flagship booths |
| Lighting | Track lighting, accent lighting; trade-show grids enable both |
| Flooring | Custom carpet (often branded color), sometimes raised flooring |
| Power and data | High-amp power, networked drops, AV cabling planned |

### 4.2 Booth Touchpoint Categories

- Pre-show: email invitations, press appointments, ABM target invitations
- At-show: booth design, demo experience, ambient music/sound, scent (rare but powerful), refreshment
- Collateral: printed materials (one-page leave-behind, deeper folder, full brochure), QR code lead capture, business card collection
- Swag: branded items — quality calibrated to brand tier (executive brands: Moleskine, Field Notes, premium pens; mass enterprise: t-shirts, water bottles)
- Post-show: follow-up sequence, account-based marketing trigger

### 4.3 Conference Speaking and Sponsorship

When the brand sponsors or presents at conferences:
- Stage backdrop branding
- Slide template system (must integrate with corporate template system)
- Speaker support materials (talking points, slide notes)
- Networking event branding (cocktail hours, dinners, breakfasts)
- Industry award ceremony sponsorship architecture

## 5. Sales Enablement System

The sales enablement system is the **most-used set of branded artifacts** in any B2B organization. It must be designed.

### 5.1 Pitch Deck System

| Tier | Specification |
|------|---------------|
| Master pitch deck | Long-form (40-80 slides) covering the full narrative |
| Stakeholder-specific decks | Variants for CFO, CTO, line manager, etc. |
| Demo-day deck | Short-form (10-15 slides) for first-meeting use |
| Account-specific deck | Template adapted per account with customer logos, customer-specific value props |
| Roadshow deck | For executive-led customer meetings |

Each deck is built on a template system that integrates with corporate document templates (consistent typography, color, slide masters).

### 5.2 Sales Battlecards

Per competitor: pricing, positioning, common objections, response templates. Battlecards are often digital, in tools like Highspot, Showpad, Seismic.

### 5.3 ROI Calculators and Tools

Spreadsheet or web-based tools that customers can self-serve or sales can co-build:
- Cost-benefit calculator
- Total cost of ownership comparison
- Payback period model
- Risk assessment worksheet

These are branded touchpoints; specifications must include the layout, color palette, typography, and brand mark application.

### 5.4 Proposal and Contract Templates

Branded templates for:
- RFP responses (often 50-200 pages — must be design-systematic)
- Statement of work
- Master service agreement (legal-led but design-supported)
- Change order
- Quarterly business review document

### 5.5 Customer-Facing Content System

- Case study template (long-form)
- One-page case study (executive summary version)
- Customer reference card (for internal sales use)
- Customer logo wall (web + slide-deck format)
- Customer testimonial video (production spec, brand integration spec)

## 6. Account-Based Marketing (ABM) Touchpoints

For enterprise sales, ABM touchpoints are increasingly central:
- Customer-personalized landing pages (each major account gets its own URL with custom content)
- Direct-mail drops (high-touch printed pieces sent to target executives)
- Executive briefing center experience (in-office or virtual)
- Customer advisory board materials and event design
- Personalized video outreach (Loom, Vidyard) — branded preview/end-card

## 7. Digital Enterprise Touchpoints

### 7.1 Corporate Website

Hierarchy of pages:
- Homepage — corporate identity expression
- Product/service pages — feature, benefit, technical depth
- Customer pages — case studies, logo wall, testimonials
- Resource pages — white papers, webinars, e-books, blogs
- About — corporate identity story, leadership, careers
- Investor relations — for public companies
- Pressroom — for press and analysts
- Compliance/security pages — for regulated industries

Each section follows a system. Specify nav structure, breadcrumb behavior, footer architecture, language switching for multi-region brands.

### 7.2 Customer Portal / Self-Service

For SaaS or platform brands:
- Authenticated experience design
- In-app brand expression (often more restrained than marketing site)
- Help center / knowledge base brand integration
- Support ticketing brand integration

### 7.3 Email Marketing System

| Touchpoint | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Newsletter | Cadence (monthly, quarterly), content mix (curated content, owned content, customer stories), template system |
| Nurture sequence | Multi-stage automated emails per persona / stage |
| Sales outreach | Templates for prospecting, follow-up, re-engagement |
| Transactional | Welcome, confirmation, password reset — also branded |
| Webinar invitation | Pre-event sequence, day-of, post-event |
| Event-based | Conference invitations, customer dinner invitations |

## 8. Compliance and Regulatory Touchpoints

For regulated industries (financial services, healthcare, legal), regulatory touchpoints have specific requirements:
- Disclosure footers in every email and document
- Compliance review workflow for marketing materials
- Public filings branded consistently
- Investor materials (10-K, 10-Q, annual reports) — major design investment
- Press releases — regulated format for material events

The brand-touchpoint specification must address compliance touchpoint requirements when relevant.

## 9. Voice in B2B Touchpoint Copy

B2B touchpoint copy:
- **Quantify outcomes**: "Reduce procurement cycle from 90 to 45 days," not "Make procurement easier"
- **Cite proof**: "Used by 8 of the Fortune 100," not "Industry-leading"
- **Acknowledge sophistication**: B2B buyers are skeptical of marketing language — measured, specific, evidence-based
- **Use industry-appropriate jargon**: Domain fluency is a credibility signal at appropriate level
- **Provide depth**: Long-form is acceptable and often expected (white papers, comprehensive case studies)
- **Maintain consistency across channels**: Corporate, sales, marketing, customer success all aligned in tone

## 10. Governance and Production Discipline

B2B touchpoints often involve:
- Multiple internal stakeholders (marketing, sales, product, legal, compliance, IT)
- External agencies and production partners
- Long production timelines (90-180 days for major collateral)
- Periodic refresh cycles (annual at minimum for major artifacts)

The brand-touchpoint specification must include:
- Approval workflow per touchpoint category
- Production timeline per category
- Refresh cadence per category
- Version control discipline (which version is current, which retired)
- Asset management system integration (DAM — Digital Asset Management)
