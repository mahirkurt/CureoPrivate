## 2. Material Atlas Elevation Doctrine

Luxury touchpoints are constructed from material decisions, not graphic decisions. The visual identity becomes nearly irrelevant against the material substrate; the material is the brand.

Derval's principle (*Designing Luxury Brands*, Ch. 8): luxury customers read materials more accurately than they read words. A heavyweight paper stock conveys more than a clever tagline. A hand-finished edge conveys more than a multi-page brand narrative.

The brand-touchpoint specification must therefore include a **material atlas** at unprecedented depth.

### 2.1 Paper and Print Substrates

| Touchpoint | Mass Market | Luxury Specification |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Business card | 250-350 gsm uncoated | 600-1,000 gsm cotton or duplex Mohawk Superfine; debossed not printed for brand mark; edge color optional |
| Letterhead | 80-100 gsm | 120-180 gsm laid or cotton; watermark optional for super-luxury |
| Envelope | Standard kraft or white | Lined envelopes; tissue interleaving; wax seal optional for ceremonial correspondence |
| Hangtag | Cardboard die-cut | Cotton or linen-weave card stock; hand-stitched or hand-tied attachment |
| Care card | Lightweight insert | Heavyweight, often letterpress on cotton; sometimes hand-signed |
| Catalog | Coated stock + perfect bound | Uncoated heritage stock + Smyth-sewn binding; sometimes hand-bound for super-luxury |

Specify: paper mill name, weight (gsm), finish (uncoated, laid, vellum, cotton), color (against named swatch), printing method (offset, letterpress, digital — letterpress preferred for marks), edge treatment (cut, deckled, gilt, painted).

### 2.2 Fabric and Soft Goods Touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Luxury Specification |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Tote bag | Cotton canvas (minimum 280gsm) or saddle leather; handles riveted or hand-stitched; brand mark embossed or printed in oil pigment |
| Pouch | Suede, calfskin, or fine linen; full grain leather preferred over corrected grain; lined |
| Dust bag | Premium felt (compressed wool) or sateen cotton; drawstring with chord; brand mark embroidered not printed |
| Garment bag | Wool felt or cotton sateen; full-length with handle; pocket detail |
| Ribbon | Grosgrain, silk satin, or jacquard; weight matches brand register; tied not heat-sealed |

### 2.3 Packaging Materials

Luxury packaging is **architectural**. Specifications must address:
- Outer carton: rigid set-up box (not folding carton), minimum 1,200 gsm grayboard with paper wrap
- Wrap paper: weight, finish, color against named swatch, brand mark debossed or foil-blocked
- Tissue: weight, color, often two layers in different weights
- Ribbon: width, material, color, tying instruction
- Bow or seal: hand-tied detail spec, never machine-formed
- Inner structure: foam insert, satin insert, custom-cut to product silhouette
- Closure: magnetic clasp, magnet-and-ribbon, or sealed; never simple flap
- Shopper (carry bag): rigid handles, rope handles, or rolled-paper handles depending on tier

### 2.4 Signage Materials

| Touchpoint | Mass Market | Luxury Specification |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Exterior signage | Vinyl on aluminum, illuminated acrylic | Cast bronze, hand-carved stone, sandblasted glass, hot-foil-stamped wood |
| Window vinyl | Adhesive vinyl | Hand-etched glass, painted directly on glass with traditional sign-painter techniques |
| Wayfinding | Printed acrylic plaques | Cast metal, hand-painted, or engraved heritage materials |
| Storefront identity | Printed or applied | Three-dimensional letters in cast bronze, wood, or stone — hand-fabricated |

## 3. Retail Atmosphere Choreography

Luxury retail is not a place of transactions; it is a **stage for ritual encounters**.

### 3.1 Spatial Doctrine

| Element | Mass Market | Luxury Specification |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| Sales floor density | Maximum product per square meter | Minimum — spacious circulation, generous setbacks |
| Product display | Maximum visibility | Curated selection; some items behind glass; visit-by-appointment for highest tier |
| Lighting | Bright, uniform | Variable, often dimmer; spot lighting on hero items; warm color temperature (2700-3000K) |
| Sound | Background music | Often silence, or commissioned soundscape; never licensed pop |
| Scent | Ambient or none | Bespoke scent calibrated to brand; consistent across all locations |
| Temperature | Building default | Specified — cooler in summer (suggests luxury indulgence), warmer in winter (comfort) |
| Floor material | Tile, polished concrete | Hardwood, stone (marble, travertine), wool carpet — never synthetic |
| Wall material | Painted drywall | Wood paneling, fabric-wrapped panels, raw plaster — never gloss paint |
| Ceiling | Drop tile or exposed | Coffered, beamed, or hand-finished — emphasizes craftsmanship |

### 3.2 Ritual Choreography

Each customer interaction is a ritualized encounter. Specify:

- **Welcome ritual**: how customer is greeted (named if known, by appointment); time-to-greeting (immediate but not rushed)
- **Discovery ritual**: how product is presented (brought from back, never grabbed off shelf; placed on velvet or felt; gloves used when appropriate)
- **Consideration ritual**: how customer interacts with product (offered seating, refreshment — water, sparkling water, sometimes champagne or coffee depending on time and culture)
- **Purchase ritual**: how transaction is conducted (away from common area, often in private salon or seated area; paperwork minimal; payment unobtrusive)
- **Wrapping ritual**: how product is packaged in front of or for customer (often performed deliberately; paper folded with attention, ribbon tied with care)
- **Farewell ritual**: how customer is escorted out (to door at minimum, sometimes to car; doorman culture in flagship locations)

### 3.3 Sales Associate Specification

Luxury sales associates are **product of training**:
- Multi-year tenure expected; high retention culture
- Product knowledge equivalent to brand historian
- Conversational fluency in multiple languages typical
- Personal styling, hospitality, and discretion as core competencies
- Customer relationship management — many associates have personal client books spanning years
- Compensation structure decoupled from transaction (or capped commission) to align with relationship-building rather than closing

The touchpoint specification must include sales associate role specification + training curriculum brief + customer relationship management workflow.

## 4. Digital Touchpoints — Luxury Discipline

Digital touchpoints in luxury follow specific restraint rules:

### 4.1 Web Atmosphere

| Element | Mass Market | Luxury Specification |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| Page load | Performance-optimized | Performance-optimized but visually allows generous loading transition |
| Hero imagery | Multiple, rotating | Often single, held; image rotation slow if any |
| Color palette | Brand-saturated | Often near-monochrome; color held in restraint |
| Typography | Brand-defined, varied | Two faces maximum; size variations restrained |
| Whitespace | Functional | Architectural — large blocks of white; layout breathes |
| Navigation | Persistent | Minimal, sometimes hidden behind hamburger; flat hierarchy |
| Imagery | Product-focused | Atmospheric — products in context, ateliers, materials, hands at work |
| Animations | Often present | Restrained; never bouncy or playful |
| Video autoplay | Common | Rare; if used, muted and ambient |
| Cart visibility | Persistent header | Sometimes hidden until item added; cart called "bag" or "selection" |
| Checkout flow | Optimized for speed | Optimized for confidence; address validation, multiple payment methods, hand-written-style order confirmation |

### 4.2 Email and Correspondence

| Touchpoint | Mass Market | Luxury Specification |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Email cadence | Weekly | Monthly or by occasion; never high-frequency |
| Email tone | Promotional | Editorial — newsletter rather than marketing |
| Subject line | Action-oriented | Restrained, often single-noun ("Spring") |
| Visual treatment | Designed templates | Often minimalist or text-led; hand-feeling layouts |
| Personalization | Name-merge typical | Sometimes literal hand-signed correspondence from sales associate to private clients |
| Postal correspondence | Rare | Maintained for high-tier clients — printed catalogs, hand-addressed envelopes, occasional gifts |

### 4.3 Social Media

| Channel | Luxury Use Pattern |
|---------|-------------------|
| Instagram | Primary — curated atmosphere, ateliers, craftsmanship, occasional product |
| Pinterest | Often present — aspirational pinning by users; brand-curated boards |
| LinkedIn | For corporate brand of the parent group; not for product brands |
| Facebook | Often skipped — demographic mismatch increasingly |
| TikTok | Cautious entry, when used: behind-scenes, craft-focused; rarely promotional |
| YouTube | Long-form atmospheric content; documentary-style maker films |
| Twitter / X | Rare; if used, restrained official voice for press relations |

## 5. Ephemera and Ritual Objects

Luxury brands invest in **ephemera that customers preserve**:
- Annual catalogs as collectible objects (heritage stock, archival binding)
- Branded books — coffee-table heritage volumes, often published by maison itself
- Calendars, agendas, journals as gifts to top clients
- Limited-edition objects (sometimes non-product — pencils, candles, scarves) for client gifts
- Notecards and stationery branded for client correspondence

Specify: which ephemera the brand produces, what occasion, who receives, production specification.

## 6. Voice in Touchpoint Copy

Touchpoint copy in luxury contexts:
- **Avoid superlatives**: not "the most refined" or "the finest" — quality is shown, not declared
- **Avoid urgency**: no "limited time," "last chance" — scarcity is structural, not manufactured
- **Use proper nouns**: name the atelier, the artisan, the source material, the year of founding
- **Prefer specific over general**: "Hand-stitched in Florence by master saddlers since 1948" beats "Crafted with passion"
- **Avoid customer-flattery**: no "for the discerning gentleman" — implies the customer needs reassurance
- **Use measured time references**: "since 1854," "three generations," "the spring collection" — not "limited time only"

## 8. Production Partner Implications

Luxury touchpoints typically require specialty production partners that mass-market projects do not engage:

- **Letterpress and engraving studios** for invitations, business cards, ceremonial paper
- **Hand-binders** for catalogs, books, special editions
- **Saddler-stitchers** or master leatherworkers for soft goods
- **Cast bronze and stone fabricators** for exterior signage
- **Hand sign painters** for storefront identity
- **Bespoke fragrance houses** for retail scent design
- **Sound designers** for retail soundscape composition
- **Heritage paper mills** (Mohawk, Crane, Fabriano, Arches) for paper specification

Production partner briefs in luxury contexts must include heritage credentials, sample swatches, and visit-and-review checkpoints. Many luxury maisons maintain decade-long production partnerships.
