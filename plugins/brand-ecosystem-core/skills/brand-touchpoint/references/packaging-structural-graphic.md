# Packaging — Structural & Graphic

Reference loaded when packaging is in scope (physical product brand). Packaging is often the highest atmospheric load touchpoint for product brands.

## Why packaging is decisive

Packaging is the brand's most-encountered physical artifact. Customers see it before purchase, hold it at purchase, unbox it after purchase, and often re-encounter it during use. For consumer product brands, packaging is more frequently encountered than logo, website, or advertising.

Packaging operates in two distinct registers simultaneously:
- **Structural packaging**: the physical container (form, materials, opening mechanism, protective function)
- **Graphic packaging**: the visual identity expression on that container (logo, color, typography, copy, imagery)

Excellent packaging integrates structural and graphic decisions as one design problem.

## Structural Packaging Decisions

### Form factor

The package shape and proportion. Standard options vary by category:

| Category | Common structural options |
|---|---|
| Beverages | Bottle (glass/PET), can (aluminum), carton (Tetra Pak), pouch |
| Beauty / cosmetics | Jar, tube, pump bottle, dropper bottle, compact, ampoule |
| Food (dry) | Box, bag, jar, can, pouch |
| Food (fresh) | Tray + film, clamshell, vacuum pack, deli paper |
| Apparel | Folded + tissue + box, hanging garment bag, dust bag |
| Electronics | Hard box (premium), softpack (consumer), tray + sleeve |
| Personal care | Tube, bottle (pump or flip-cap), bar with sleeve |
| Supplements / consumer health | Blister + carton, bottle + carton, pouch |
| Spirits / wine | Glass bottle + closure system, optional outer presentation box |

Form decision is strategic, not standard. Distinctive form factor signals investment in the brand experience.

### Material strategy

Material decisions carry atmospheric weight:

**Paper / paperboard / corrugated**:
- Atmospheric signals: craft, considered, sustainable, sometimes premium (depending on weight + finish)
- Sustainability: high (recyclable, biodegradable in standard forms)
- Cost: low to medium
- Finishes: uncoated kraft (craft), matte coated (clean), satin coated (premium-accessible), high gloss (mass-market)

**Glass**:
- Atmospheric signals: weight, premium, considered, heritage
- Sustainability: high (infinitely recyclable, but heavy = transport carbon)
- Cost: medium to high
- Form variation: very wide (custom mold investment is substantial)

**Plastic (PET, HDPE, PP)**:
- Atmospheric signals: contemporary, accessible, light
- Sustainability: contested (depends on type, recycling infrastructure)
- Cost: low to medium
- Best uses: where weight or breakage requires plastic

**Metal (aluminum, tin)**:
- Atmospheric signals: contemporary, durable, sometimes premium
- Sustainability: high (aluminum infinitely recyclable)
- Cost: medium
- Best uses: beverages, cosmetics premium, gift packaging

**Composite / hybrid**:
- Often necessary for technical requirements (Tetra Pak for shelf-stable liquids)
- Atmospheric signals: technical, sometimes mass-market
- Sustainability: often lower (multi-material harder to recycle)

**Sustainable / specialty**:
- Bagasse, mushroom-based, seaweed-based, recycled-content materials
- Atmospheric signals: aligned with sustainability positioning
- Cost: medium to high, scaling improving
- Brand fit: must be authentic to brand stance

### Closure / opening mechanism

How does the customer access the product? This is often where premium and mass-market diverge:

- Screw cap (standard, functional)
- Flip-top (everyday, casual)
- Pump (cosmetics, personal care)
- Cork + capsule (wine, premium spirits)
- Magnetic closure (premium boxes)
- Ribbon tie / hand-tied (craft, gift, luxury)
- Tear strip (functional, single-use)
- Press-fit lid (premium boxes)

The opening moment is brand experience. A premium brand opening like cheap retail packaging breaks atmospheric coherence.

### Protective function

Structural packaging must protect the product. Specifications:

- Product fragility level
- Transit conditions (shipping, retail handling)
- Storage conditions (humidity, temperature, light sensitivity)
- Shelf life requirements
- Stacking strength (for retail display)

Protective function constrains material + form choices.

## Graphic Packaging Decisions

### Layout architecture

What information appears where on the package surface? Standard zones:

- **Primary display panel** (PDP): the face the customer sees first at retail
- **Secondary display panel**: visible at angles or back of package
- **Nutrition / ingredient panel** (food/beverage): mandated content
- **Legal / regulatory panel**: barcodes, country of origin, certifications
- **Brand story panel** (premium / craft brands): copy investment area

For each, document hierarchy, treatment, and content.

### PDP design discipline

The primary display panel is the visual hero. Design principles:

- **Logo placement**: typically top third or center; rarely bottom
- **Product name / variant**: distinct from logo, clearly readable from shelf distance
- **Hierarchy**: brand → product name → variant → benefit/descriptor
- **Color application**: brand color leads; product-specific color secondary
- **Imagery / illustration**: deliberate use (product photo, abstract pattern, illustration, or no imagery)
- **Whitespace**: more whitespace = more premium signal

Shelf reading discipline: the PDP must communicate the brand + product within 1 second of customer attention. Test PDPs at 3-meter distance.

### Variant systems

When the brand has multiple products / SKUs, the variant system carries them coherently:

**Color-coded variant system**: each variant has dedicated color; rest of design consistent
**Pattern-coded system**: variant identified by pattern within consistent layout
**Type-coded system**: variant name typography differentiates
**Photo-coded system**: variant product imagery differentiates

The variant system is more complex than individual SKU design. It must work for current SKUs AND scale for future additions.

### Copy on packaging

What text appears on the package? Discipline:

**Required by regulation** (legal):
- Nutrition facts (food/beverage)
- Ingredient list
- Allergen declarations
- Country of origin
- Net weight / volume
- Manufacturer / distributor address
- Best-by / use-by dates
- Barcode

**Brand-discretionary**:
- Brand story / paragraph
- Founder note
- Product preparation / usage instructions
- Ingredient sourcing detail
- Sustainability declarations
- Awards / certifications
- Social handles / website
- QR code (with care)

**Anti-discretionary** (avoid):
- Marketing hyperbole on the package itself
- Multiple competing taglines
- Award badges accumulated over years
- Overcrowded back-panel "brand story"

Less copy is almost always better. Restraint signals confidence.

### Bilingual packaging

For multilingual markets:

- Some jurisdictions mandate two languages (Türkiye: TR + sometimes EN export; Canada: EN + FR; Belgium: NL + FR + sometimes DE)
- Language hierarchy: regulatory-required language gets primary or equal treatment
- Typographic design must accommodate both without ad-hoc adjustment
- For premium / craft brands, multilingual treatment is a craft signal

## Packaging Specification Document

For each package SKU, the specification includes:

### Structural specifications
- Dimensions (height × width × depth)
- Material (primary substrate + secondary materials)
- Weight (target)
- Capacity (volume or count)
- Closure mechanism
- Stacking specifications
- Drop test / transit test requirements

### Graphic specifications
- Final artwork (vector, layered)
- Color callouts (Pantone + CMYK + spot colors)
- Substrate-specific adjustments (color shifts on different materials)
- Bleed + safety margins
- Die-line / structural file
- Barcode + variable data placement

### Production specifications
- Printing method (offset, flexo, digital, screen, hot foil, deboss)
- Finishing (varnish, lamination, foil, deboss, emboss, spot UV)
- Substrate sourcing (paper grade, plastic grade)
- Production partner brief

### Regulatory specifications
- Required certifications (FDA, CE, food contact, recycled-content claims)
- Required label content per jurisdiction
- Country-specific compliance

## Sustainability considerations

Modern brands increasingly require sustainability stance in packaging:

- **Recyclability**: actually recyclable in the market the brand sells in (varies by jurisdiction)
- **Recycled content**: % post-consumer recycled material
- **Bio-based materials**: plant-based or bio-derived alternatives
- **Reduced material**: lightweighting, structural efficiency
- **Refill / reuse programs**: closed-loop systems
- **Compostability**: home or industrial compost certification

Sustainability claims require substantiation. Greenwashing risks legal exposure + brand damage.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Logo + standard tube | "Branded" packaging = stock packaging with logo applied | Design structural + graphic as one problem |
| PDP overcrowding | Every claim, every cert, every benefit on the front | Restrict PDP to brand + product + one core message |
| Variant inconsistency | Each SKU designed independently, system breaks | Establish variant rules before designing any SKU |
| Cost-driven cheapening | Premium positioning, mass-market materials | Match material to positioning; deprioritize if needed |
| Ignored opening moment | Premium brand opens like budget product | Specify the opening experience |
| Sustainability inauthenticity | Sustainability claims unsupported by structural choices | Align claims with actual practice |
