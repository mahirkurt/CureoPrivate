# Signage & Environmental Graphics

Reference loaded when signage is in scope (physical brand presence). Covers primary signage, wayfinding, vehicle livery, environmental graphics.

## Primary Signage

The most public touchpoint a physical brand has. Signage is read at distance, in varied light, by passersby who weren't seeking the brand.

### Signage types

**Building signage**:
- Façade signage (illuminated or non-illuminated, mounted or projecting)
- Window vinyl / window etching
- Awning + sign integration
- Rooftop signage (for visibility at distance)
- Heritage building signage (constrained by local regulations)

**Storefront signage**:
- Above-door signage
- Hanging blade sign (projecting at 90° from façade)
- Window decals
- Sandwich board / A-frame (street-level)
- Entry mat

**Office building signage**:
- Lobby identification
- Floor directory
- Suite identification
- Elevator buttons / floor numbers

### Material + technique selection

| Material | Atmosphere signal | Production complexity | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brass / bronze, lettering | Heritage, permanence, prestige | High | Very high |
| Painted wood | Craft, considered, vernacular | Medium | Medium (requires maintenance) |
| Powder-coated steel | Industrial, contemporary, durable | Medium-high | High |
| Cast aluminum | Substantial, custom, modern | High | Very high |
| Acrylic + vinyl | Contemporary, budget-flexible | Low-medium | Medium |
| Illuminated channel letters | Visible at night, retail standard | High | High |
| Neon / faux neon | Distinctive, atmospheric, ages | Medium-high | Medium |
| Direct application on architecture | Integrated, architectural | High | Very high |

Selection follows atmospheric intent + practical constraints (lighting, weather, regulatory).

### Sign specifications

For each sign, document:

- **Dimensions** (with viewing distance considered — sign-to-viewer-distance ratio)
- **Materials** (substrate + face + mounting)
- **Color application** (Pantone for finishes; CMYK for printed elements)
- **Typography** (face + weight + size + tracking — signage typography differs from print typography)
- **Mounting method** (mechanical, adhesive, structural)
- **Lighting** (front-lit, back-lit, edge-lit, non-illuminated; warm white, neutral white, brand-specific tone)
- **Permits required** (local jurisdiction requirements)
- **Maintenance protocol** (cleaning frequency, refinishing schedule)

### Viewing distance + sign size

Standard rule: 1 inch of letter height per 10 feet of viewing distance for daytime reading.

- 30 ft viewing → 3" letters minimum
- 100 ft viewing → 10" letters
- Building signage seen from 500 ft → 50"+ letters

Apply this to sign sizing decisions.

### Signage typography vs print typography

Print typography and signage typography are different disciplines:

- **Tracking**: signage typography often needs tighter or looser tracking than print (medium-distance reading)
- **Weight**: signage typically uses heavier weights than print (legibility at distance + in varied light)
- **Aperture**: signage typefaces with open apertures read better at distance
- **Cap height vs x-height**: signage typically increases x-height for medium-distance reading

The brand's identity typography may or may not work for signage. If not, a complementary signage face is specified.

## Wayfinding

Indoor or outdoor navigation system. For brands with substantial physical presence: offices >1 floor, retail >800 sq ft, hospitality, cultural institutions, mixed-use environments.

### Wayfinding components

- **Identification signs**: this is where you are
- **Directional signs**: this is how to get there
- **Information signs**: here is what's offered
- **Regulatory signs**: this is what you must / must not do

### Wayfinding hierarchy

Three-level hierarchy typical:
- Primary: building-level (lobby directory, floor identification)
- Secondary: floor-level (corridor signs, area identification)
- Tertiary: room-level (door numbers, room functions)

Each level uses consistent format with size + emphasis cascading from primary to tertiary.

### Specifications per wayfinding type

- **Material standardization** across all signs in system
- **Color coding** (if used for categorization — e.g., floor color = department color)
- **Typography hierarchy**
- **Pictogram system** (if used) — custom or standard (ISO 7001)
- **Mounting heights** (standard ADA / DDA / TS 9111 specifications)
- **Multi-language** if applicable (primary + secondary language treatment)
- **Universal design** considerations (visual + tactile for accessibility)

### Wayfinding production

Wayfinding systems are typically produced by specialty signage fabricators. The brief includes:
- Full sign inventory (every sign + location)
- Sign-type specifications
- Material specifications
- Production schedule
- Installation specifications
- Maintenance handoff

## Vehicle Livery

For brands with fleet — delivery, service, sales, hospitality shuttles, mobile units.

### Vehicle livery decisions

**Coverage level**:
- Minimal: door logo + side text
- Partial: hood + doors + back, some color blocking
- Full wrap: entire vehicle surface treated as brand canvas

**Color strategy**:
- Brand color as vehicle color (paint, not wrap) — most durable, most committed
- Branded wrap on neutral vehicle — flexible, replaceable
- Branded elements on company-color vehicle — middle ground

### Specification elements

- Vehicle types in fleet (van, truck, car, motorbike, specialized)
- Wrap material (cast vinyl, calendared vinyl, paint)
- Color matching (Pantone or custom paint formulation)
- Typography (similar discipline to signage — distance reading)
- Logo placement (each side, hood, rear, front grille if appropriate)
- Window treatment (perforated vinyl, clear, untreated)
- Contact information placement
- Mandatory information (legal name, registration if required by jurisdiction)
- Fleet variant rules (sales vs service vehicles distinguished how?)

### Production partner brief

Includes:
- Vehicle make + model + year (for fitment)
- Vector artwork at correct scale
- Material specifications
- Installation timeline
- Replacement schedule (wraps typically refreshed every 3-5 years)
- Decommissioning protocol (vehicle retired or transferred — how is livery removed)

## Environmental Graphics

Graphics integrated into the architecture or interior of brand spaces. Distinct from signage in that environmental graphics are atmospheric rather than functional.

### Environmental graphic types

- **Large-format wall graphics**: brand statements, manifestos, hero imagery
- **Window graphics**: pattern application, transitional graphics, frosting
- **Floor graphics**: directional markers, brand patterns, area identification
- **Ceiling graphics**: hanging signs, banners, illuminated elements
- **Architectural integration**: brand color in built elements, custom millwork
- **Patterns**: repeating brand pattern as wall covering, fabric, flooring

### When to use environmental graphics

- Retail interior establishing brand atmosphere
- Office reception and shared spaces (employee + visitor atmospheric experience)
- Hospitality common areas (lobby, restaurant, spa)
- Cultural institutions (exhibition design, donor walls)
- Event spaces (booth design, conference branding)

### Specifications

- **Surface preparation** (clean, primed, sealed)
- **Material** (paint, vinyl, fabric, screen print, hand-painted)
- **Application method** (machine-applied vinyl, hand-painted, embedded into architecture)
- **Durability expectations** (permanent vs refreshable vs temporary)
- **Maintenance** (cleaning protocol, refresh schedule)

## Bilingual Signage

For brands operating in multilingual environments (Türkiye, UAE, Switzerland, Canada, multilingual cities):

- Both languages on every public sign typical
- Hierarchy: brand uses primary language with larger / lead position
- Visual treatment: both languages typographically integrated (not added as afterthought)
- Right-to-left languages (Arabic, Hebrew): require either mirrored layout or special handling

Multilingual signage that handles language well signals competence. Multilingual signage that handles language badly (cramped second-language treatment, inconsistent type sizes, English-first defaulting in non-English markets) signals carelessness.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Signage as logo enlargement | Sign = blow up the logo to building size | Signage requires typography, layout, distance design |
| Material misalignment | Premium brand with vinyl signage | Match material to atmospheric intent |
| Ignored permit constraints | Sign design fails municipal regulations | Verify constraints before design |
| Wayfinding inconsistency | Building uses 3 different sign systems on 3 floors | Govern wayfinding centrally |
| Vehicle livery as billboard | Vehicle wrap maximizes brand message density | Treat vehicle as brand atmosphere, not ad space |
| Environmental graphic overload | Every wall has a brand message | Restraint; let architecture breathe |
