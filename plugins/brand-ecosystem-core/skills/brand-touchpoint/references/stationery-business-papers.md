# Stationery & Business Papers

Reference for the stationery touchpoint category. Loaded when stationery is in scope (default for almost all brands).

## What stationery covers

- Business card
- Letterhead
- Envelope (multiple sizes)
- Compliments slip / "with compliments" card
- Presentation folder
- Email signature (digital stationery)
- Letter close + signature treatment

## Why stationery still matters

In a digital-first era, stationery seems anachronistic. But:
- For professional services / B2B, physical stationery still appears at key moments (in-person meetings, leave-behinds, gifts, formal correspondence)
- Quality stationery signals "we have invested in our presentation" in a way digital cannot
- Stationery is often the second-most-handled brand artifact (after packaging for product brands)
- Email signatures are stationery in digital form — and most brands handle them poorly

For luxury, professional services, hospitality, and craft brands, stationery is a heavy atmospheric load touchpoint.

## Business Card Specification

### Mandatory elements
- Card dimensions (standard or custom)
- Paper stock specification (brand, weight, finish, color)
- Printing method (offset, letterpress, foil, deboss, emboss, digital)
- Layout (information hierarchy, alignment, spacing)
- Color application
- Typography (face, size, weight, color)
- Logo treatment (size, position, color)
- Mandatory information (name, role, contact)
- Optional information (tagline, social, secondary office)

### Card dimensions options
- Standard US: 3.5" × 2" (88.9mm × 50.8mm)
- Standard EU: 85mm × 55mm
- Standard credit-card: 85.6mm × 53.98mm
- Square: 65mm × 65mm or 88mm × 88mm
- Slim: 84mm × 35mm
- Folded: 85mm × 110mm folded to 85mm × 55mm

Standard dimensions are recommended unless brand atmosphere specifically benefits from non-standard size (square or slim for differentiation).

### Paper stock guidance
- 300 gsm minimum for professional impression
- 350-400 gsm for premium impression
- 600+ gsm or duplexed for super-luxury impression
- Uncoated for craft/considered atmosphere
- Coated (matte or gloss) for clean/contemporary atmosphere
- Colored core stock for distinctive cards

### Printing technique selection
- Offset 4-color: standard quality, cost-efficient
- Letterpress (deboss): tactile, craft signal, mono or limited color
- Foil stamp: prestige signal, single color overlay
- Emboss / deboss: tactile differentiation
- Hot foil: high prestige signal
- Edge painting: distinctive finishing detail
- Spot UV: contemporary tactile element

Selection follows atmospheric intent. Luxury brands often combine 2-3 techniques (e.g., letterpress + foil edge); craft brands often use single distinctive technique (letterpress only).

### Layout discipline
- Information hierarchy: name > role > contact
- Logo placement: typically corner or full-bleed; not centered unless atmospheric reason
- Whitespace: generous; cluttered cards signal anxiety
- Alignment: typically left or center; consistent across card
- Two-sided cards: front carries logo + identity; back carries contact info
- Single-sided cards: simpler, lower production cost, suitable for many brands

### Mandatory information
- Person's name
- Role / title
- Email address
- Phone number (mobile preferred; office optional)
- Office address (mandatory if customer visits expected)
- Website URL

### Optional / brand-discretionary
- Tagline
- Social handles
- Secondary office address(es)
- Languages spoken (multilingual contexts)
- QR code (use with care; QR codes age the design)

## Letterhead Specification

### Mandatory elements
- Paper size (A4 standard for EMEA/Asia, US Letter for US)
- Paper stock (same family as business card, often same paper)
- Logo position (typically top — left, center, or right)
- Address block (typically footer or top-right)
- Color application
- Typography for letterhead vs body
- Margin specifications (typically 25mm + extra for printer margin)

### Letterhead options
- **Full letterhead**: pre-printed paper with logo + address; letter typed onto it
- **Digital letterhead**: PDF/Word template with embedded logo + address
- **Combined**: pre-printed for formal correspondence; digital for routine

Most brands today use digital primarily. Pre-printed letterhead remains for high-stakes correspondence (legal documents, formal proposals, founder communications).

### Body typography guidance
- Often same typeface as identity system, in document-friendly weight
- Body sizes: 10-11pt for letters, 11-12pt for longer documents
- Line spacing: 1.4-1.6
- Generous margins

## Envelope Specification

### Standard sizes
- DL (110×220mm): standard letter envelope
- C5 (162×229mm): A5 documents folded once, or A4 folded twice
- C4 (229×324mm): A4 documents flat or folded once
- US #10 (4.125"×9.5"): US standard letter envelope

### Envelope variants
- Window envelope (address visible through window from letter)
- Closed envelope with printed address
- Self-seal vs gummed
- Liner / interior pattern (for branded interior — luxury touch)

### Production specifications
- Paper stock (often same family as letterhead but lighter — 100-120 gsm)
- Logo placement (typically front, often back flap)
- Color application
- Return address placement

## Email Signature Specification

The most-encountered stationery item in modern practice. Often the most poorly handled.

### Mandatory elements
- Sender name
- Role
- Company / brand name
- Email
- Phone
- Website

### Brand-discretionary elements
- Office address(es)
- Social media links
- Pronouns
- Booking link / calendar link
- Legal disclaimer (some sectors require)
- Recent content link (book, podcast, talk)

### Format options

**Plain text** (preferred for many brands):
- Renders consistently across email clients
- Loads fast, no image embedding
- Resilient to forwarding
- Format:
```
Name | Role
Company
+phone
email@brand.com
brand.com
```

**Lightweight HTML** (acceptable for visual brands):
- Logo (small, transparent PNG, max 200px wide)
- Brand color accent (single color, restrained use)
- Typography: web-safe (Arial, Georgia) or system fonts
- No background images, no large graphics

**Heavy HTML** (avoid):
- Multi-column layouts
- Large embedded images
- Animated GIFs
- Social icons as image array
- Background colors
- Custom typography requiring font embedding

Heavy HTML signatures break across email clients, slow loading, and signal trying-too-hard.

### Governance
- Single template adopted across team
- Generator tool or copy-paste template provided
- Mobile signature shorter version provided
- Update protocol when staff changes

## Compliments Slip / Presentation Folder

### Compliments slip
- Small card (often 99×210mm — third of A4)
- Used for short notes accompanying physical sends
- Carries logo + brand name + space for handwritten note
- Production: same paper family as letterhead

### Presentation folder
- A4+ pocket folder for client presentations, proposals, documents
- Material: heavy stock (300-400 gsm minimum) or branded cardboard
- Pockets: typically 2 (inside left + right)
- Closure: tucked, flap, or magnetic
- Embellishments: foil, deboss, edge painting per brand
- Used for: client onboarding kits, proposal delivery, conference handouts

For B2B / professional services, presentation folders remain valued. For DTC consumer, less essential.

## Stationery Coherence Check

After spec'ing all stationery, validate:

- All stationery uses same paper family (different weights OK, fundamentally different paper not OK)
- Typography is consistent across stationery
- Logo treatment is consistent
- Color application is coherent
- Production techniques harmonize (letterpress business card + offset letterhead = OK; letterpress business card + cheap inkjet letterhead = NOT OK)

The stationery system is itself a touchpoint. Coherent stationery system signals care; incoherent stationery system signals lack of attention.

## Production partner brief

For each stationery piece, the production partner brief includes:

- Final artwork file (vector, CMYK or Pantone, with bleed)
- Paper stock specification (brand + weight + finish + color)
- Printing method
- Quantity
- Finishing (cutting, scoring, folding if applicable)
- Color callouts (Pantone numbers, spot colors)
- Proofing requirements (digital proof, press proof if budget permits)
- Production timeline
- Delivery specifications

Brief is sufficient for the partner to quote and execute without follow-up clarification.

## Bilingual stationery

For multilingual brands:

**Business cards**: often two-sided, one language per side. Order language and ID treatment consistent.

**Letterhead**: typically primary language for body, secondary language for footer details. Some brands maintain separate letterhead templates per language.

**Email signature**: language by recipient. Some sender systems auto-switch by recipient domain.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Cheap stationery, expensive identity | Logo cost $50K, business cards cost $0.05 each | Re-budget; stationery is identity application |
| Information overload card | Card lists every contact method, multiple addresses, tagline, social, QR | Reduce ruthlessly; what does the recipient need? |
| Generic stationery | Logo dropped onto Vistaprint template | Specify materials + production deliberately |
| Email signature disasters | Multi-image signatures, animated GIFs, oversized logos | Move to plain text or restrained HTML |
| Stationery inconsistency | Each employee uses different signature, different business card design | Govern centrally; provide tools |
