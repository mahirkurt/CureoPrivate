# Sensory Dimensions Beyond Visual

Reference loaded when the brief involves multi-sensory brand expression — hospitality, retail, luxury, fine dining, automotive, premium consumer, branded environments. Operationalizes Derval's sensory-mix doctrine plus the broader sensory branding literature.

## Why visual identity must extend beyond visual

A customer encountering a hotel doesn't experience the brand visually only — they smell the lobby, hear the ambient sound, touch the materials, taste the welcome amenity. A customer entering a flagship store doesn't experience the brand visually only — same multi-sensory bandwidth. A customer driving an automotive brand experiences sound (engine note), tactile (steering wheel, materials), olfactory (interior scent character).

For these brands, visual identity that addresses only the visual layer underserves the customer experience. The brand-visual output must declare specifications for sonic, tactile, olfactory, and where relevant gustatory dimensions.

## The five sensory layers

### Visual
Already covered by brand-visual's core SOP. Includes color, typography, logo, imagery, motion, environmental graphics.

### Tactile
The brand's touch character:

- **Material weights**: how brand artifacts feel in the hand
- **Material textures**: smooth, textured, coated, uncoated, soft-touch, ribbed
- **Surface finishes**: matte, satin, gloss, embossed, debossed, foiled
- **Temperature signaling**: cool metal vs warm wood vs neutral plastic
- **Resistance / give**: hard surfaces vs cushioned, soft fabrics vs structured

Tactile signature applies to: packaging (high tactile load), business cards (often-overlooked tactile moment), retail / hospitality surfaces, product hardware finishes.

Tactile decisions read luxury / craft / contemporary / accessible faster than visual decisions in many contexts.

### Olfactory
The brand's scent character:

**Stance options**:
- **Deliberate signature scent**: a specific olfactory identity (Aman properties' specific scent, Le Labo retail signature)
- **Ambient scent**: neutral, clean, generally pleasant — no specific signature
- **Anti-scent**: deliberate avoidance of fragrance (especially in spaces where customers may have sensitivities)
- **Material-derived scent**: scent comes from materials used (wood, leather, paper) without added fragrance

A brand's olfactory stance applies to: physical retail, hospitality property, packaging-opening moments, gift-with-purchase rituals.

### Auditory
The brand's sound character:

**Components**:
- **Sonic logo / brand audio identifier**: 2-7 second audio signature
- **Music palette**: playlist character or commissioned music
- **Voice character**: in podcasts, videos, customer service, in-store announcements
- **Ambient sound design**: in retail / hospitality / digital experiences
- **Product sounds**: notification sounds, UI sounds, mechanical sounds (where relevant — automotive engine note, appliance feedback sounds)

Auditory applies to: digital products, video / podcast presence, retail environments, hospitality properties, automotive, broadcast presence.

### Gustatory
For sectors where taste is relevant (food, beverage, hospitality, fine dining):

- **Flavor signature**: signature dish / drink / amenity
- **Taste philosophy**: minimalist / layered / regional / experimental
- **Tasting protocol**: how the brand introduces flavors (sequencing, pairings, narratives)
- **Welcome amenity character**: the first taste of the brand experience

For hospitality, gustatory is a major brand dimension. For retail and most B2B, irrelevant.

## Sonic identity specifications

When sonic identity is in scope:

### Sonic logo brief
- Duration: typically 2-5 seconds (rarely longer)
- Character: derived from brand personality (Sage archetype → restrained, considered tonality; Magician → transformative, ambient)
- Where it appears: video intros / outros, app launch, podcast bumpers, video calls hold music
- Variations: long form (full sonic logo) + short form (mnemonic) + extended form (intro music)

Production handoff to audio designer / sound designer, not produced inside brand-visual.

### Music palette brief
- For retail / hospitality / events with curated music:
- Genre constraints
- Energy curve (morning vs evening, arrival vs dwell)
- Vocal vs instrumental preferences
- Era / regional constraints
- Anti-list (what never plays)

### Voice character
- For podcasts, videos, voice assistants:
- Speaking pace
- Vocal warmth / coolness
- Accent / regional flavor (or neutral)
- Formality register
- Sample voice references (for talent casting)

### Sonic anti-patterns
| Anti-pattern | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Generic stock music | Brand uses untreated stock music in every video |
| Sonic logo absent | Heavily video-presented brand without audio mnemonic |
| Voice inconsistency | Different voices in different brand audio touchpoints |
| Music genre drift | Retail music shifts based on staff preference |

## Olfactory specifications

When olfactory is in scope:

### Scent stance declaration
- Deliberate signature scent / ambient pleasant / anti-scent / material-derived only
- Rationale tied to brand atmosphere

### Signature scent brief (if deliberate signature)
- Olfactory family: floral / woody / fresh / oriental / gourmand / etc.
- Specific notes (top / middle / base)
- Atmosphere descriptor
- Where it appears (retail, packaging, hotel rooms, etc.)

Production handoff to perfumer / olfactory designer, not produced inside brand-visual.

### Olfactory anti-patterns
| Anti-pattern | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Olfactory inconsistency | Different scent in different locations of same brand |
| Overpowering scent | Customers experience scent as imposition |
| Cheap fragrance signaling | Mass-market scent reads as "trying" |
| Allergy-blind scent | No accommodation for scent-sensitive customers |

## Tactile specifications

### Material atlas (per touchpoint expected to have tactile dimension)
- Recommended materials by touchpoint
- Material weight specifications
- Surface finish specifications
- Temperature character
- Alternative materials (for sourcing / budget flexibility)

### Tactile anti-patterns
| Anti-pattern | Symptom |
|---|---|
| Visual-only premium | Premium-priced brand with budget tactile experience |
| Tactile inconsistency | Premium business card with budget letterhead |
| Material-finish mismatch | Mismatch between substrate and finish (foiling on inappropriate substrate) |

## Gustatory specifications (hospitality, food, beverage)

### Flavor signature brief
- Signature dish / drink / amenity character
- Flavor profile (sweet / savory / bitter balance)
- Regional / international character
- Anti-list (what never appears)

### Welcome amenity specification
- First-encounter taste of the brand
- Format (drink, bite, both)
- Service timing (arrival, post-arrival, room delivery, etc.)

## Multi-sensory coherence

The five sensory dimensions must compose coherently. A brand cannot have considered visual + tactile + olfactory if the auditory layer is generic stock music.

Coherence test: a customer experiencing the brand multi-sensorially carries away a unified atmospheric impression. If one sensory layer breaks the impression, the brand experience is incoherent.

When brand-visual outputs sensory specifications across multiple layers, the coherence check runs across all specified layers.

## Bilingual + cultural sensitivity in sensory branding

Sensory norms vary across cultures:

- Olfactory preferences differ by region (Middle East tolerates / prefers stronger olfactory presence; East Asia often prefers cleaner / neutral)
- Auditory preferences differ (music genre tolerances, ambient sound expectations)
- Tactile expectations differ (in some cultures, weighty business cards signal seriousness; in others, lightweight cards signal contemporary)

When the brand operates across cultures, sensory specifications acknowledge these:
- Core sensory identity (stays consistent)
- Local sensory adaptation (varies by market within constraint)

## Anti-patterns across sensory layers

| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Visual-only thinking | Brand identity treats only visual layer | Add tactile + relevant other layers |
| Sensory token addition | One scent + one playlist added late | Sensory must be integrated from foundation |
| Inconsistency at scale | One flagship location does sensory; chain doesn't | Govern sensory across full footprint |
| Cultural insensitivity | One sensory standard imposed globally | Adapt sensory within brand constraint |
| Production handoff confusion | Sonic brief written by visual designer without audio expertise | Use sensory specialists for production |
