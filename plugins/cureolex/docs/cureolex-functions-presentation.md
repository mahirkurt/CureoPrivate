Cureolex is a **jurisdiction-shaped** protocol. You designate a country; the nine work modes then run against that country’s sources, hierarchy, and drafting conventions. **Turkey is the flagship example** — the deepest national stack, and the instance that already proves the full nine-mode package. Other countries are reached through official legislation portals, treaties, EU and ICH instruments, OECD better-regulation material, and comparative law. Coverage is **not** equal everywhere; this deck does not pretend that it is.

Roche appears in this deck as a **regulated-industry stakeholder** — a research-based marketing-authorisation holder that may sit in the reform room as a consulted body, a commenter on a kılavuz, or a commissioner of a landscape, görüş, RIA, or comparative pack. Roche does **not** own Cureolex. The protocol will not write promotional copy for Roche or for any other holder.

Official names of instruments and institutions stay in their original language only when they are proper names in an example (Resmî Gazete, TİTCK, SUT, 5210 sayılı Yönetmelik, Anayasa, TBMM, gerekçe, and peers).

This deck explains **functions** and **artefacts**. It does not describe software, systems, or how the work is engineered.

## Slide 1 — What Cureolex is

### On-screen text

**Cureolex**
A health-policy and legislative-reform agent for a **designated country**

Name a jurisdiction. The same nine work modes run against **that** country’s official sources, legal hierarchy, and drafting conventions — from a new regulation to a parliamentary bill.

**The hierarchy (generic)**

constitution → statute / decree → regulation → circular / guidance

**Turkey example (flagship national stack)**

Anayasa → kanun / Cumhurbaşkanlığı Kararnamesi → yönetmelik → tebliğ → genelge

**Today:** Turkey is the complete national stack. Other countries are used as comparators and, where official portals exist, as the file’s home jurisdiction for landscape, comparative, and impact work. Drafting the full package in a non-Turkish legislative idiom is the **destination**.

**Aim:** the same protocol, the same artefacts, for whichever country is named.

**What you receive is a file for human sign-off — not a published rule.**

A research-based company (Roche is the worked industry example) may **commission or use** the same file. It does not own the protocol.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

Cureolex is a reform protocol for health, medicines, devices, and reimbursement law. Its job is to turn a policy question — “we need an advanced-therapy regime”, “this reimbursement line is unworkable”, “does this draft survive our form book?”, “we have twelve days to answer a TİTCK kılavuz” — into a complete, citable package that a ministry lawyer, a medicines regulator’s drafting unit, a parliamentary adviser, or a marketing-authorisation holder’s regulatory and market-access counsel in the **named country** can actually use.

It is not a search engine and not a substitute for that country’s official gazette. It reads official sources, maps the current landscape, drafts or amends operative text in the article order that jurisdiction requires, writes the justification that jurisdiction requires, and then stress-tests the result for substance, form, language, and reasons. Every package ends with a coverage statement (what was searched, what was empty) and a confidence label. A human with authority must still decide.

Turkey is one worked example of that country-specific mode — a mature instance, not the product’s identity. In the Turkish file the constitutional frame is Anayasa md. 17 (personal inviolability), md. 56 (right to health and the State’s duty), and md. 90 (treaties in force have the force of law; in a conflict over fundamental rights and freedoms, the treaty prevails), together with ICESCR Article 12 and OECD-style better regulation. Another designated country brings **its** constitution, **its** enabling statutes, **its** gazette, and **its** drafting rules. The protocol will not invent a citation to fill a gap. If a source cannot be reached, it says so and points to the official portal.

Roche, in this deck, is a **user persona**: a research-based holder of medicines and diagnostics that public materials describe as combining those two businesses and as engaging public authorities on access, personalised healthcare, and the regulatory framework (Roche public materials, 2025–2026 — About / strategy and public-policy pages). That is why Roche can commission a Cureolex file. It is not why Cureolex exists. The designated country remains the first fact on the brief.

### What the function does

Orients the room: Cureolex is a **reform workshop for a named jurisdiction**, not a Turkey-only database, not a court, and not a company-branded tool.

### What the user walks away with

A shared definition: *a signed-off drafting and analysis package for the designated country*, ready for institutional review — including review by an MAH that did not write the rule.

## Slide 2 — Who it is for — and who it is not for

### On-screen text

**For** (in the **designated country**)

- Legislative and regulatory counsel in the health ministry, the medicines and devices regulator, the public payer, and related bodies
- Parliamentary advisers preparing a health statute or bill
- Institutional lawyers writing a formal opinion letter
- Clinicians and HTA advisers who must attach evidence to a **rule**, not treat a patient
- **Industry regulatory, market-access, and health-policy teams** of a research-based marketing-authorisation holder (Roche is the example) when they must produce a consultation answer, a class-level listing comment, or an evidence annex for a **norm**

**Turkey names, as examples**
Ministry of Health · TİTCK · SGK · TBMM advisers · a professional chamber writing a görüş · a Roche affiliate (or any MAH) answering a kılavuz or a SUT draft as a consulted stakeholder

**Not for**

- An individual lawsuit, a reimbursement-denial file, or a constitutional complaint by a named person
- A malpractice defence
- Promotional or marketing copy, a leave-behind, or a speaker-bureau slide
- A substitute for the lawyer of record — including Roche Global or affiliate legal
- A filing with TİTCK, EMA, or any other competent authority
- A price, a SUT outcome, or an HTA recommendation dressed as law

Human review is mandatory on every output.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

The protocol is scoped to **norm production**: writing or changing the rule that will bind many people in the country that was named. That is a different craft from representing one patient, one company, or one physician. If someone arrives with “my reimbursement was refused” or “draft a petition to the constitutional court”, the correct answer is to send them to case-work counsel — not to produce a fake lawsuit on reform stationery.

The same boundary applies to promotional material. Screening a leave-behind, a speaker-bureau slide, or a CME programme is a compliance-of-promotion task. Cureolex will not write advertising, and it will not bless a marketing claim as if it were a regulation. That limit is load-bearing when the commissioner is a research-based MAH. Roche public policy materials describe constructive dialogue with government officials and industry associations (Roche public materials, 2025 — public-policy page). Dialogue about a **rule** is in scope. Copy that would be used to promote a named product is not.

Clinicians are welcome in the room when they are advising on *the rule*: for example, whether an off-label-use circular should require a multidisciplinary board, or whether an advanced-therapy regulation should define hospital exemption. Industry medical-policy colleagues are welcome on the same terms — evidence standards for an orphan listing, or whether a companion diagnostic must be named in the same reimbursement line as the class. They are not using Cureolex as a treatment algorithm or as a brand plan.

Every finished package states, in plain language, that a human must review it and that it is decision support, not an official legal opinion of the State. In a Turkish file that sentence is about the Republic; in any other designated country it is about that State. For an MAH, a second sentence is implied: affiliate and Global legal still sign.

### What the function does

Draws the professional boundary before any drafting begins — including the industry side of the table.

### What the user walks away with

A clear yes/no: *is this a reform file for the named country, or should it go to litigation, promotion review, or the company’s own filing counsel?*

## Slide 3 — A research-based MAH in the reform room

### On-screen text

**Roche is the worked example of a research-based holder — not the product owner.**

Cureolex is a file that Roche, or any other marketing-authorisation holder, could **commission**. The State still writes and publishes the rule.

**Who typically speaks (affiliate functions, not a Roche org chart)**

| **Market access / reimbursement** | SUT-like annex amendment table; HTA-clock and listing-rule RIA comments; transitional / acquired-rights language |
| **Medical policy / government affairs** | Görüş letter; comparative pack (ATMP, CDx co-regulation, early access, orphan evidence); parliamentary evidence annex |

**What lands on the desk is always a reform artefact**
A görüş · a three-column amendment table · a RIA comment annex · a comparative gap matrix · a COMPLY scorecard on someone else’s draft

**What does not land**
A promotional claim · a price · a TİTCK or EMA dossier · a guaranteed listing

### Speaker notes / full meaning

A research-based company sits in the reform room as a **stakeholder**, not as the legislator. Roche’s public materials describe two facts that matter for commissioning, and only those two: the company combines pharmaceuticals and diagnostics and aspires to personalised healthcare along the care pathway; and it treats public authorities as partners on access, innovation, and the regulatory framework (Roche public materials, 2025–2026 — About / strategy and public-policy pages). Those public themes explain *why* an affiliate might need a CDx-and-medicine co-regulation note, an early-access RIA annex, or a görüş on a kılavuz. They do not license Cureolex to recite Roche strategy, to name pipeline assets, or to write as if Roche owned the protocol.

The four functions on the slide are the ordinary split inside a research-based MAH. They are not a published Roche Turkey organigram; do not present them as one. Legal and regulatory counsel care whether a secondary instrument invents a duty the parent statute never created. Regulatory affairs care whether yesterday’s TİTCK kılavuz is being applied as if it were a yönetmelik, and whether trial, ethics, and pharmacovigilance duties are determinate. Market access cares about the living reimbursement schedule, the HTA or listing clock, and the transitional sentence that protects patients already on treatment. Medical policy and government affairs care about the letter that must go back inside the consultation window. Each of those jobs maps onto a Cureolex mode and an artefact. None of them maps onto a leave-behind.

Honesty about the consultation clock belongs here. In the Turkey example, 5210 sayılı Yönetmelik gives **consulted public bodies and professional organisations** fifteen days, and treats silence as assent. A Roche affiliate is not, by that fact, a 5210 mandatory consultee. The affiliate may be invited as an interested party, may answer through an industry association or a professional chamber, or may face a **different** comment window on a TİTCK kılavuz. Cureolex writes the görüş in the form the receiving institution expects, states which clock applies, and does not pretend the company is TİTCK or the Ministry. If the designated country were Germany, the analogue is a Stellungnahme in a BMG / BfArM / G-BA consultation; if the Union, a response to a Commission or EMA public consultation; if Japan, a comment on an MHLW ordinance or PMDA notice — same stakeholder role, local form.

### What the function does

Places the research-based MAH on the correct side of the table: consulted stakeholder and commissioner of artefacts, never author of the State’s rule and never author of promotional copy.

### What the user walks away with

A **role card**: which affiliate function commissions which artefact, and which requests must be sent to company legal, pricing, or promotion review instead.

## Slide 4 — The problem a reform file must solve

### On-screen text

A health rule that will survive scrutiny usually needs **all** of the following, not just operative text:

| Need | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Correct legal level | A regulation cannot invent a penalty the statute did not authorise |
| Article order and form | That country’s legislative-technique rules prescribe structure, citations, and language |
| Justification | General reasons, then article-by-article reasons — without repeating the article |
| Comparison | Old text beside new text, for any amendment |
| Landscape | What already exists, including circulars that never reached the gazette |
| Foreign and treaty benchmark | Treaties in force for that country; EU, ICH, and peer practice where relevant |
| Impact | Who pays, who benefits, what else was considered |
| Honesty | No invented sources; empty searches declared |

**Turkey example of the form book:** 5210 sayılı Yönetmelik (structure, citation form, gerekçe discipline).

**Pharma-stakeholder picture of the same completeness**
Hospital exemption cannot be smuggled into a notice if only a statute can carry it. A companion diagnostic that is required for a listed class must appear in the **same** legal instrument family as the listing, or the file must say why it does not. A SUT cut without a transitional sentence is an acquired-rights problem, not a budget footnote. A TİTCK kılavuz that never reached Resmî Gazete is still part of the landscape ANALYZE must find.

**Cureolex is built to produce that whole file — for the country you name.**

### Speaker notes / full meaning

Health regulation fails in predictable ways in every system. A beautifully written article that has no upper-norm authority is cancelled. An amendment that does not show the old wording next to the new wording is sent back. A draft that copies another country’s language without checking whether the designated State is a party to the relevant treaty, or whether its own regulator already issued guidance on the same point, creates silent conflict. A justification that merely restates the article (“this article sets the purpose”) fails the usual drafting rule: the reasons must *explain why*, not reprint the rule. In the Turkey example that rule is 5210 sayılı Yönetmelik md. 23; another country has its own equivalent in a style manual, a cabinet handbook, or standing orders.

From the industry side of the table the same failures have familiar names. An ATMP “hospital exemption” copied from Union language into a Turkish tebliğ, without an enabling kanun, is an annulment gift. A reimbursement line that names an oncology class but is silent on the companion test that the labelled use requires will be argued over in listing committees rather than in the gazette. A reference-price or additional-monitoring list used as if it were living law, without a vintage, is a coverage failure. Roche public HTA materials argue that assessment systems should be locally appropriate, transparent, and able to handle new paradigms such as personalised healthcare (Roche public materials — Position on Health Technology Assessment). Cureolex does not adopt that position as its own policy. It does insist that a RIA or a SUT justification name the local HTA or listing method that actually applies, rather than importing a foreign appraisal as if it were domestic law.

The protocol exists because those pieces are usually produced in different rooms, on different days, from different piles of paper. Cureolex keeps them in one file: operative text, general and article justification, comparison table, impact annex, bibliography, coverage statement, and confidence. The room should leave this slide knowing that “please write a regulation” is an incomplete request. The complete request is “please produce a reform package that this country’s legislation office or parliamentary services can actually process” — or, if the commissioner is an MAH, “please produce the annex we can attach to a görüş without pretending we are the State.”

### What the function does

Names the completeness standard before the nine modes are introduced.

### What the user walks away with

A checklist of what “done” means for a health-law file in this protocol, in the designated country — including the industry-facing holes (soft law, CDx, acquired rights) that a gazette-only reading would miss.

## Slide 5 — Nine work modes at a glance

### On-screen text

| Mode | In one line, you receive… | Pharma-stakeholder picture |
|---|---|---|
| **DRAFT** | A new health rule, in that country’s article order, with general and article justification | ATMP regime including hospital exemption, if the parent law can carry it |
| **AMEND** | A change to a living instrument, with old/new comparison tables | SUT (or peer listing) line for a CDx-linked class; acquired-rights / transition |
| **ANALYZE** | A map of the current legal landscape and its weak points | The TİTCK kılavuz that never reached Resmî Gazete; off-label vs labelled use |
| **COMPLY** | A fitness check against **that country’s** drafting and justification rules | Would this tebliğ survive 5210 (or the local form book) if we are the consultee? |
| **OPINE** | An institutional or expert opinion letter, in the standard form | The 15-day (or local) görüş — MAH or association as consulted body |
| **RIA** | Ex-ante impact: costs, benefits, alternatives, OECD-style better regulation | Early-access or orphan-evidence tebliğ; pricing/procurement **landscape**, not a price |
| **COMPARATIVE LAW** | Foreign + treaty + ICH benchmark, gaps, and policy options | Union ATMP + German implementing law; ICH E6/E8 as scientific floor, not a dossier format |
| **PARLIAMENTARY BILL** | A legislative-bill dossier for that country’s parliament | Trial/ethics statute; secondary use of health data; a levy or agency a notice cannot create |
| **EX POST** | After-the-fact evaluation of a rule already in force | Early-access scheme or additional-monitoring list, 12–36 months on |

**Turkey examples in the same row:** a new yönetmelik under 5210; a SUT amendment table; a 5210 fitness scorecard; a görüş letter; DEA / BEF; a TBMM kanun teklifi under Anayasa md. 88.

Modes can be chained. A typical reform uses several, not one. A typical MAH commission uses ANALYZE → OPINE or AMEND, not DRAFT of the State’s rule.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

These nine modes are the whole functional surface. They are not software menus; they are kinds of professional work that a health ministry already recognises. DRAFT and AMEND produce operative text. ANALYZE and COMPARATIVE LAW produce the map and the peer picture that justify the text. COMPLY is the quality gate before a file leaves the building — against the designated country’s form book, not against a universal checklist with a Turkish name. OPINE is how another institution — or a consulted stakeholder — answers a consultation. RIA is the ex-ante impact file that better-regulation practice expects. The parliamentary bill is a different legal animal: it follows that country’s constitution and standing orders, not the executive’s regulation-drafting manual. EX POST is the look-back, usually 12–36 months after entry into force.

A user does not have to know the mode name. “We need an advanced-therapy regulation from scratch” is DRAFT. “Change this reimbursement line” is AMEND. “Is this draft fit to circulate?” is COMPLY. “How does Germany and the Union do hospital exemption?” is COMPARATIVE LAW. “The kılavuz arrived; we have a comment window” is OPINE, with ANALYZE in the background. The protocol chooses the mode from the intent, then delivers the artefact that mode is designed to produce. Turkey is where the full nine-mode chain can already be run as a national file. Elsewhere, landscape, comparative, and impact work can already sit in that country as home jurisdiction where official portals exist; the destination is the same nine artefacts in that country’s own legislative idiom.

An MAH should hear the chain as **commissioning language**, not as a promise that Cureolex will file on their behalf. Roche public materials on regulatory reliance treat ICH scientific standards as the common floor that makes trust between regulators possible (Roche public materials, 2024–2025 — regulatory-reliance story). That is a COMPARATIVE and justification use of ICH. It is not an eCTD publishing service.

### What the function does

Gives the room a map of all nine deliverable types, with one industry picture per mode.

### What the user walks away with

A one-page menu they can use to commission work: *name the country, name the mode, name the artefact* — including from the industry side of the table.

## Slide 6 — DRAFT — a new instrument from a blank page

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
There is no adequate instrument, or the existing one must be rewritten rather than patched.

**Typical subjects (universal problems)**
An advanced-therapy (ATMP) regime · hospital exemption · companion-diagnostic and medicine co-regulation · a new scheduling or listing notice · an organisational rule at decree level · a named-patient / compassionate / early-access scheme that today lives only in a kılavuz

**What is written**

1. Operative text in **that country’s prescribed article order** — typically purpose → scope → legal basis → definitions → substance → sanctions (only if the parent statute allows) → amended/repealed provisions → transitional → entry into force → execution → annexes
2. **General justification** — why the instrument exists
3. **Article justification** — why *this* article exists (it must not repeat the article)
4. Bibliography of verified sources
5. Coverage statement and confidence

A regulation will not invent duties, taxes, organisation, or penalties that the parent statute or decree did not authorise.

**Turkey example:** 5210 md. 15 article order; genel gerekçe and madde gerekçesi; enabling chain often 1262, 3359, 7223, and Presidential Decree No. 4 on TİTCK.

**Who commissions DRAFT:** usually the ministry or the regulator. An MAH more often commissions ANALYZE, COMPARATIVE LAW, and OPINE on someone else’s draft — unless asked to propose model articles as a consultation annex, clearly labelled as a proposal.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

DRAFT is the core production mode. It begins by locating the upper-norm chain in the designated country: constitution, then the enabling statute or decree, then any sibling regulation that already occupies the field. It also looks sideways: repealed historical texts, ministry circulars that never reached the gazette, regulator guidance, and at least a first foreign and treaty benchmark so the new text is not written in a vacuum.

The article order is not a style preference. A reader — and later a court — must find purpose, scope, basis, and definitions before the operative duties. Scope must not be a paraphrase of purpose. Definitions are kept in the order that jurisdiction requires (in the Turkey example, alphabetical, and not re-sorted when a new term is added later). Sanctions, fees, and new administrative structures are the most common illegal overreach: a regulation that “creates” an administrative fine the statute never created is a gift to an annulment action.

The two justification documents are separate. The general justification tells the story of the field: what exists, what broke, what science or international standard moved, what the draft will change. Each article justification explains the *reason* for that article — the gap, the court signal, the ICH or treaty alignment — and is forbidden from reprinting the article text.

Worked picture, with Turkey as illustration: an ATMP yönetmelik would define advanced-therapy medicinal products, set the competent unit inside TİTCK, describe the dossier path, and — if hospital exemption is wanted — say who may manufacture under exemption, for which named-patient use, and on what reporting. It would not invent a new criminal offence. A second DRAFT picture, closer to a diagnostics-plus-medicines company, is a co-regulation notice: when a companion diagnostic is required for a labelled use, the instrument states whether the test is authorised under the medicines rule, the device/IVD rule, or both, and who is competent. Roche public Europe materials describe medicines, diagnostics, and clinical-trial frameworks as poorly interconnected and call for predictable, risk-based co-regulation (Roche public materials, 2025 — Europe drug-device innovation story). Cureolex will not paste that essay into a Turkish gerekçe. It will ask whether the designated country’s enabling chain can actually carry a joint rule, and it will say so if it cannot.

If the designated country were Germany, the same ATMP file would have to sit under the German medicines statutes and the competent higher federal authority, in German legislative technique; if the United Kingdom, under the Human Medicines framework and the chosen statutory-instrument or primary-legislation vehicle; if Japan, under the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act and the ministerial-ordinance / cabinet-order distinction, including regenerative-medicine vehicles where they exist. Those are how the **same problem** would be housed — not a claim that a finished German, British, or Japanese ATMP package already ships as a product. The package that leaves the room is a draft plus justifications, not a published gazette page, and not a Roche product authorisation.

### What the function does

Writes a new health instrument from the enabling law downward, in the official article order of the designated country, with both layers of justification.

### What the user walks away with

A **stand-alone draft file**: operative articles, general justification, article justification, source list, coverage, confidence — ready for internal legal review and later COMPLY / RIA if the instrument type requires them. An MAH that proposed model articles still walks away with a **proposal**, not a filing.

## Slide 7 — AMEND — change what is already in force

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
The instrument stays. Specific articles, phrases, annexes, or transitional rules must change.

**Change types the file can express**

- Replace an article (old wording in quotation marks, then the new article)
- Replace a phrase throughout
- Add an additional article or a transitional article
- Repeal a provision

**What you receive**

| Artefact | Role |
|---|---|
| Amending text | The instrument that will be published |
| Comparison table | Current text \| proposed text \| short reason |
| Phrase table | If only words change, where and how often |
| Article justification | Why this change, not a reprint of the new wording |

**Universal problem, Turkey illustration:** a reimbursement-schedule line (SUT) — adding a class or a test, clarifying a report duration, updating a directorate name after a reorganisation, writing the **transitional** sentence that protects ongoing treatment (acquired rights).

**Pharma-stakeholder picture:** a SUT (or peer listing) amendment for a **CDx-linked oncology class** — the medicine class and the required test in one delta, with a vintage on any TİTCK list used as evidence. Not a brand leave-behind. Not a price.

If the designated country were Germany, the United Kingdom, or Japan, the same delta would attach to that country’s living reimbursement or listing instrument (social-code / G-BA rules; NHS / NICE commissioning and tariff text; NHI listing), in **that** amendment technique — again as the shape of the file, not as a product already on the shelf.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

AMEND is how most live health files actually move. A reimbursement schedule is patched rather than rewritten; a licensing regulation is updated article by article; a regulator’s notice refreshes a list. The legal technique is strict. A serious form book distinguishes a full article replacement from a phrase replacement, and it requires the amending instrument to show the old text and the new text so that the legislation office, consulted institutions, and later a court can see the delta.

The comparison table is not optional decoration. For an amendment it is expected to travel with the consultation letter and with the submission to the centre of government. A reviewer who cannot see “what was” beside “what will be” cannot give a meaningful opinion. The table’s third column is a short reason — clinical evidence, a renamed directorate after a reorganisation, a court-driven clarification — not a second copy of the article.

The protocol also reconstructs the **cumulative** previous wording, not only today’s consolidated text. That matters in health law because a reimbursement line may have been rewritten five times; the justification for the sixth change should know what the fifth already tried. Citation dates follow the official form of the designated country.

Worked picture (Turkey): a SUT amendment that adds delamanid beside bedaquiline, fixes the specialist-report duration at six months, and replaces an obsolete department name with the current Halk Sağlığı Genel Müdürlüğü. The walk-away file is the amending tebliğ, the three-column table, and the article gerekçe. It is not a new SUT from scratch — that would be DRAFT.

Industry picture of the same mode: a research-based MAH (Roche as example) does not ask Cureolex to “get our product onto SUT.” It asks for a **class-level** delta: the labelled use that requires a companion diagnostic, the test’s place in the annex, the reporting duration, and the transitional rule if a previous line is narrowed. Acquired rights and non-retroactivity are legal tests, not goodwill. A line that cuts an ongoing treatment without a transitional article will fail COMPLY even if the budget office likes the saving. Roche public HTA materials emphasise locally appropriate, transparent assessment of new paradigms (Roche public materials — Position on Health Technology Assessment). The Cureolex artefact is the comparison table and the justification that a listing committee or a 5210 consultation can read — not an HTA model, and not a prediction that SGK will list.

Reference-price and public-procurement rules, when they are the living instrument, are also AMEND work: map who sets the list, what vintage was read, what the amending sentence would do. The protocol will not recommend a Roche list price or a tender strategy.

### What the function does

Classifies the change, writes it in the official amendment technique of the designated country, and proves the delta in tables.

### What the user walks away with

An **amendment dossier**: amending articles + comparison table (+ phrase table if needed) + article justification, suitable to attach to a consultation or a centre-of-government submission — or, for an MAH, to attach to a görüş as a proposed delta, clearly labelled as such.

## Slide 8 — ANALYZE — map the landscape before you write

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
Before a reform, or when someone asks: “is this instrument lawful, complete, and still fit **here**?”

**Seven questions the map answers** (in the designated country)

1. Does every duty sit under a valid upper norm?
2. Do the articles still serve the stated purpose?
3. Are obligations determinate enough for a court (legal-certainty case-law)?
4. Are citations in the official form, and do they point to living law?
5. Is the article architecture intact?
6. Does a regulation or notice try to do what only a statute may do?
7. Is the legal language of that jurisdiction clear enough to apply?

**Plus:** foreign / EU / ICH deviation, court and doctrine signals, and an annulment-risk reading.

**ANALYZE must find the rule that never reached the gazette**
A TİTCK or Ministry kılavuz, a board decision, an additional-monitoring list, a named-patient practice, an ethics-committee circular. Soft law is labelled soft law. Empty search is not “no such rule.”

**Pharma-stakeholder pictures:** off-label vs labelled use · compassionate / named-patient / early access · pharmacovigilance / additional monitoring / RMP as **legal landscape** · orphan evidence standards · secondary use of health data as a **regulatory file**.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

ANALYZE is the pre-reform diagnosis. It is the mode that stops a team from drafting a second regulation on a field that is already occupied by a notice, a ministry circular, and a regulator guideline that never made it into the gazette. The output is a hierarchical findings note, not operative articles. For a research-based MAH this is often the first — and sometimes the only — mode worth commissioning before a görüş is written. The affiliate that answers a kılavuz without first mapping kanun, yönetmelik, tebliğ, SUT, and the unpublished kılavuz family will comment on a ghost.

The seven dimensions follow the tests a legislation office actually uses: upper-norm fit, purpose-and-content fit, determinacy (a rule that leaves the citizen guessing will not stand), citation technique, article structure, the legality of obligations (a secondary instrument cannot create new burdens the parent law did not allow), and language. Beside those sit a peer-deviation note (how far the domestic rule sits from a Union, ICH, or named-peer benchmark) and a court-and-doctrine filter: constitutional and administrative courts that hear health and pharmaceutical administration, private-law edges where they appear, and academic writing of that country.

The annulment-risk reading is a professional caution, not a prediction of how a future chamber will vote. It flags patterns that have already produced cancellations: undefined “as deemed appropriate” duties, penalties invented at regulation level, retroactivity, and conflict with a treaty that the constitution would prefer.

Worked picture: “analyse the current off-label-use regime.” In Turkey the map would place the enabling kanun, any TİTCK or Ministry circular, the reimbursement consequences in SUT, the absence or presence of a published list, and the certainty problem if “off-label” is used without a decision-maker, a time limit, or a registry duty. A second picture: “where does compassionate use / named-patient / early access actually live?” The map may find a kılavuz, a ruhsat practice, and a SUT silence — three ranks, not one scheme. A third: pharmacovigilance. Cureolex will map additional-monitoring lists, RMP-related duties, and reporting clocks as **the legal landscape**. It will not write Roche’s pharmacovigilance SOP. A fourth: secondary use of health data and real-world evidence. Roche public-policy materials list “how to ensure access to big data” among personalised-healthcare engagement issues (Roche public materials, 2025 — public-policy page). The Cureolex job is the **file**: which statute authorises secondary use, who consents, what the ethics committee must see. It is not a Roche data-strategy paper.

If the designated country were Germany, the United Kingdom, or Japan, the same seven questions would be asked of *those* statutes, guidance notes, and payer rules. The user does not yet receive a new circular. They receive the landscape that makes a later DRAFT, AMEND, or OPINE defensible. Landscape work is already usable with another country as **home** jurisdiction where official portals exist; that is ANALYZE’s natural first job outside the Turkish stack.

### What the function does

Reads the living hierarchy of the designated country and returns a structured legal map with risk heat, not a draft — including the kılavuz that never reached the gazette.

### What the user walks away with

A **landscape and risk note**: findings by dimension, peer gaps, court/doctrine signals, and a recommended next mode (DRAFT, AMEND, OPINE, or leave in force).

## Slide 9 — COMPLY — fitness against that country’s drafting rules

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
A draft already exists — yours or someone else’s — and must be cleared before circulation or publication.

**The gate is local**
The protocol does not apply one country’s form book to another. It asks: would this file survive the designated jurisdiction’s legislation office, consulted institutions, and later administrative courts?

**Four families of tests** (substance · form · language · justification)

| Family | Asks |
|---|---|
| **Substance** | Upper-norm authority, purpose, acquired rights, non-retroactivity, treaty alignment |
| **Form** | Article titles, paragraph hierarchy, citation form, annex and transitional rules |
| **Language** | That jurisdiction’s official legal style: short, unambiguous, no unnecessary foreign words |
| **Justification** | General and article reasons present; article reasons do not copy the article |

Each check is marked **pass**, **fail**, **conditional**, or **not applicable**.

**Turkey example — not the universal name of the gate**
**5210 sayılı Yönetmelik**: twenty-one checks in those four families. A tebliğ is not asked for a full impact file in the way a kanun or CBK draft is.

**How to read the score**

- All pass or not-applicable, few conditionals, no fails → fit to circulate
- One high-risk fail → repair before circulation
- Several fails, or a fail on upper-norm authority / illegal new burden → redesign

**Industry reading of the same gate**
An MAH uses COMPLY on the **ministry’s or TİTCK’s draft**, not as a promotional-law censor. Cureolex stops at the **norm**. Advertising, leave-behinds, and speaker copy are out of scope.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

COMPLY is the adversarial reading. It does not improve the prose for its own sake; it asks whether the file would survive the centre of government, a consulted institution, and later the administrative courts of the designated country. The first check is always upper-norm authority. If a regulation creates a duty the statute never authorised, the review stops and recommends a statute-level vehicle (the parliamentary-bill mode) or deletion of the offending article. That is not pedantry. It is the difference between a publishable instrument and an annulment.

“Conditional” is an honest middle. The authority exists but is grey; the justification is present but thin; a treaty alignment is partial. Conditional items travel with a repair suggestion. They do not silently become passes. “Not applicable” is equally honest: a notice is not always asked for a full regulatory-impact file in the way a statute or presidential decree is; a circular is not always asked for a general justification as a legal duty, though writing one remains good practice.

Language is a legal test, not a literary one. The addressee must be able to obey. “Appropriate treatment protocol”, “where necessary”, and undefined “etc.” are the phrases that later become litigation. The protocol flags them and proposes determinate replacements (who decides, in how many days, by what published list).

Industry picture: a Roche affiliate — or any MAH — receives a draft tebliğ that would create a listing duty, a hospital-exemption reporting clock, or a CDx-without-medicine gap. COMPLY is how they score that draft against 5210 (in the Turkey example) before they write the görüş. Acquired rights on a reimbursement cut, non-retroactivity, and determinate timelines are the checks that matter to patients already on treatment as much as to the holder. What COMPLY will **not** do is read the draft as advertising law. If the question is “may we say this on a leave-behind?”, the file belongs with promotion review. Cureolex will refuse that commission rather than stretch a 5210 scorecard over it.

In the Turkey example the named instrument is 5210 sayılı Yönetmelik. That name does not travel. A German, British, or Japanese file would be scored against **that** country’s legislative technique and justification rules — the destination of the protocol, with Turkey proving that a full, named fitness gate can already be run. The walk-away object is a scorecard plus a repair recipe, not a rewritten draft — unless the user then sends the repaired text back through DRAFT or AMEND.

### What the function does

Audits a reform text against the designated country’s substance, form, language, and justification rules, and refuses to hide a fail.

### What the user walks away with

A **fitness report**: scorecard, risk weight, narrative on each fail or conditional, and a concrete repair list. Publication readiness is stated in those terms, never as a government approval, and never as a promotional-law opinion.

## Slide 10 — OPINE — the institutional opinion letter

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
An institution has been sent a draft and must answer within the legal time — or an expert is asked for a reasoned opinion.

**Consultation clocks are local**
The designated country’s rules set the deadline and the meaning of silence.

**Turkey example:** consulted public bodies and professional organisations have **15 days** under 5210; silence is treated as **assent**. A research-based MAH (Roche as example) is often a **stakeholder or association member**, not a 5210 mandatory consultee — the clock that actually applies must be named.

**The letter contains**

- Official letter-head block (number, subject, addressee, reference)
- A table: article · current / proposed wording · opinion · reason
- Opinions of four kinds: accept · amend · delete · procedural objection
- A closing that a signatory can actually sign

The opinion is written **in the voice of the named institution** in the designated country — not as a private essay, and not as a brand.

**Turkey voices, as examples:** TİTCK, SGK, a TBMM commission, a professional chamber, an industry association, a Roche affiliate writing as consulted stakeholder (never as TİTCK).

### Speaker notes / full meaning

OPINE produces the document that a consultation annex expects: a structured opinion, not a three-page letter of impressions. The table is the working surface. Each row pins one article, shows the wording under review, states the institution’s position, and gives a reason that another lawyer can check — a certainty problem, a missing comparison table, a penalty that only a statute may create, a conflict with regulator guidance, a treaty point under that country’s constitution.

The protocol first reads the draft as if it were running COMPLY in the background, then filters the findings through the *mandate of the institution that is speaking*. The medicines regulator does not write the public payer’s reimbursement opinion. A ministry unit does not pretend to be the data-protection authority. A parliamentary health-committee note is written as committee advice, not as an inspector’s finding. A Roche affiliate does not write as TİTCK, and TİTCK does not write as Roche. That role discipline is what makes the letter usable in an inter-institutional file.

The consultation clock is part of the product, not a footnote. If the user is late, the protocol still writes the letter, but it states the legal consequence of silence so the signatory is not surprised. The letter is a draft for a human signatory. It is not filed, numbered, or sent. Roche public-policy materials describe a duty to provide relevant, coherent, and honest information in dialogue with public officials (Roche public materials, 2025 — public-policy page). The Cureolex artefact that matches that duty is a görüş table with checkable reasons — not a lobbying script.

Worked picture (Turkey): TİTCK is asked to comment on a Ministry tebliğ that uses “off-label” without a decision-maker or a reporting duty. The görüş table proposes determinate wording, cites the certainty line, and objects if the tebliğ tries to create a fine the parent kanun does not contain. Second picture: a TİTCK kılavuz on early access or additional monitoring is opened for comment. The Roche affiliate — or the association through which it speaks — commissions a görüş that distinguishes labelled use from named-patient use, asks where the kılavuz sits relative to 1262 and the ruhsat yönetmelik, and refuses to treat the kılavuz as if it had already been gazetted. The same letter, for a German, British, or Japanese designated body, would speak in that body’s mandate and consultation form.

### What the function does

Turns a draft-under-consultation into a signable institutional or expert opinion in the official form of the designated country — including an MAH or association as consulted stakeholder.

### What the user walks away with

An **opinion letter** (standard annex form) plus the article table, ready for the authorised person to edit, number, and sign. Cureolex does not send it.

## Slide 11 — RIA — impact before the rule is born

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
The instrument type, or the budget effect, requires an **ex-ante regulatory impact** file.

OECD-style better regulation is the common method: problem, options including “do nothing”, who is affected, costs and benefits, what cannot be quantified.

**Turkey example of the local names**
A kanun or CBK draft needs a **Düzenleyici Etki Analizi (DEA)**; a budget-touching file needs a **Bütçe Etki Formu (BEF)**. 5210 treats DEA as mandatory for those drafts. The institution prepares it; the Presidency’s strategy and budget authority finalises it.

**The impact file answers**

- What problem is being solved, with numbers where they exist?
- Who is affected (patients, professionals, firms, payers, the administration)?
- What alternatives were considered, including “do nothing”?
- Costs and benefits over a stated horizon, with optimistic / base / pessimistic cases
- What international peers and OECD-style better-regulation practice suggest
- What cannot be quantified, and must not be faked

**Pharma-stakeholder pictures:** an early-access tebliğ · orphan / rare-disease evidence standards · HTA or listing **clock** as a cost of delay · reference-price or public-procurement **landscape**. Cureolex maps who pays and what else was considered. It does **not** set a Roche price or guarantee a SUT line.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

RIA is the ex-ante cousin of EX POST. It does not write the regulation. It writes the impact annex that a serious statute or high-level decree file is expected to carry. The method is OECD-style better regulation wherever the designated country sits; the local form, the local budget annex, and the authority that *finalises* the file are country-specific.

The protocol maps affected actors before it writes numbers. An ATMP regime that looks “free” to the Treasury may be expensive for designated hospitals, for the regulator’s assessment capacity, and for the public payer if hospital-exemption products later seek reimbursement. A reimbursement-line change that looks like a small annex edit can move a large budget. An early-access tebliğ that looks like compassion can create a parallel listing path with no HTA clock, or a clock so long that the scheme is empty. Where clinical economics matter — incremental cost-effectiveness, budget impact, managed-entry agreements — those questions are treated as evidence questions and labelled as such. Where the only honest statement is “no official time series was available”, that sentence is written instead of an invented figure. Unpublished trial results and brand sales do not belong in this annex.

International comparison here is in the service of options, not of copy-paste. The Union’s HTA regulation, a German statute, a Japanese or Australian listing practice, or an OECD indicator is used to show that “do nothing”, “soft guidance only”, and “full regulation” are real alternatives with different cost profiles. Roche’s public HTA position is that there is no one-size-fits-all model and that assessment should be locally appropriate, transparent, and able to handle personalised-healthcare paradigms (Roche public materials — Position on Health Technology Assessment). Cureolex uses that only as a **question list** for the annex (which local method, which clock, which evidence standard), not as a Roche policy to be enacted. Impact work is already a natural mode when another country is the **home** jurisdiction: official statistics and peer options can be assembled even before that country’s full drafting idiom is the production language. The centre of government remains the authority that finalises the impact file. Cureolex produces the institutional draft. An MAH that commissions a RIA **comment** walks away with an annex for a görüş, not with the State’s DEA.

### What the function does

Builds the ex-ante impact and budget annexes that the designated country’s rules and OECD-style better-regulation practice expect, without inventing statistics and without setting a price.

### What the user walks away with

An **impact file** (in Turkey, a DEA and, where relevant, a BEF): executive summary, problem, options, actor map, cost–benefit, risks, sources, and an honesty note on missing data. For an MAH: the same structure as a **comment annex**, labelled as such.

## Slide 12 — COMPARATIVE LAW — peers, gaps, options

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
The question is “how do others do this, and what could the **designated country** adopt?” — not “write the domestic article yet.”

**Four question types**

1. **Benchmark** — how is X done in a named jurisdiction?
2. **Gap** — where does the home rule differ, in procedure, substance, institutions, or transparency?
3. **Policy** — is a foreign model transplantable here, and on what terms?
4. **Case law** — how have foreign courts or the Union courts treated the issue?

**Official sources the protocol can actually read today**
United States, Canada, Japan, Australia, Spain, Ireland, China, Mexico; Germany and Union implementation; Switzerland (official collection); United Kingdom judgments and parliamentary record; Union acts (existence, type, date); ICH quality / safety / efficacy / multidisciplinary guidelines; human-rights and health treaties (ICESCR, Oviedo, MEDICRIME, and peers).

**Pharma-stakeholder pictures:** Union ATMP regulation and German hospital exemption · IVD / companion-diagnostic co-regulation beside the medicine · ICH **E6** (Good Clinical Practice) and **E8** (general considerations for clinical studies) as the scientific floor for **trial and ethics rules** — never as a dossier file format · WHO-style reliance as a benchmark if the designated country is writing a reliance rule.

**You receive:** a structured comparative report, a gap matrix, and policy options — not a translated foreign statute passed off as domestic law.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

COMPARATIVE LAW is the mode that prevents two opposite mistakes: ignoring the world, and pasting a European recital into a domestic regulation as if that made it law. The report always starts from the designated country’s text and from the *age* of that text. A 1928-era statute is not compared to a 2021 Union regulation as if they were peers; the analysis says so, and offers both a historical parallel and a modern-function reading.

Jurisdiction choice is reasoned. For ATMP, the Union regulation and the German implementing statutes are usually primary; Japan or Australia may be the second peer; a contrasting model (a hard budget cap, or a system with no price control) is included when the policy question needs a boundary. For companion diagnostics, the question is co-regulation: how the medicine rule and the IVD/device rule share competence, timelines, and labelling. Roche public Europe materials describe MDR, IVDR, and clinical-trial frameworks as not well interconnected and argue for predictable, risk-based co-regulation of combined technologies (Roche public materials, 2025 — Europe drug-device innovation story). The Cureolex use of that public theme is a **gap row** in the matrix (does the designated country connect the two files?), not a transplant of Roche’s EU essay. For medical devices, the Union database is in scope; a domestic device registry may not be (see the limits slide). For data-protection or cyber-adjacent health rules, a wider European scan is available only when that optional external source is connected; if it is not, those rows stay in the table as “must be read on the official portal”, and they are never deleted to look complete.

ICH is cited as guidelines, not as domestic law. E6 and E8 are the trial-and-ethics scientific floor; Q/S/E/M more broadly are the quality, safety, efficacy, and multidisciplinary floor. They are not an eCTD publishing manual. Roche public materials on regulatory reliance treat ICH as the common scientific standards that make trust between regulators possible, alongside WHO good-reliance practice (Roche public materials, 2024–2025 — regulatory-reliance story). Cureolex will put that on the benchmark shelf when the designated country is writing a reliance, trial, or ethics rule. It will not claim that Roche’s reliance pilots are domestic law.

Every foreign citation is a verified official act or a clearly labelled secondary reading. The protocol will state the existence and type of a Union act (regulation, directive, date) rather than invent a number. Where the full consolidated foreign text cannot be retrieved, the report says so and gives the official portal path. Policy options are scored on legal fit, institutional fit, transition cost, and the designated country’s treaty-constitution rule — not on fashion.

This mode has a second job: when the user names a country other than Turkey, comparative and landscape work can treat that country as **home** and Turkey (or the Union, or ICH) as the peer. That is already in scope where official portals exist. It is not the same thing as shipping a full nine-mode drafting package in that country’s legislative idiom.

### What the function does

Builds a verified peer picture and a gap analysis that can feed DRAFT, AMEND, RIA, OPINE, or a parliamentary justification.

### What the user walks away with

A **comparative-law report**: executive findings, jurisdiction reasons, domestic baseline, peer matrix, four-way gap analysis, policy options with transition notes, and a bibliography of official sources.

## Slide 13 — PARLIAMENTARY BILL — a legislative-bill dossier

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
The change cannot lawfully live in a regulation or notice. It needs a **statute**.

**Legal home**
That country’s **constitution** and the parliament’s rules on introduction, justification, and committee referral.

The executive’s regulation-drafting manual usually **does not** govern member bills. It is used only as a drafting reference (certainty, citation form, language).

**The dossier**

1. Address to the parliamentary presidency (or equivalent) and the list of proposing members
2. General justification (need, constitution, treaties, Union, peers, courts, doctrine, effects, budget)
3. Operative bill text
4. Article justification
5. Note on debate procedure and the likely committee of first instance
6. Annexes (comparison, impact summary)

**Turkey example:** a **TBMM kanun teklifi** under **Anayasa md. 88**. 5210 **excludes** member bills from its own scope; this dossier is **not** labelled “5210-compliant.” Example subject: a structural reform of 1219 sayılı Kanun that a secondary instrument cannot carry.

**Pharma-stakeholder pictures (still a statute, still not a filing):** clinical-trial and ethics-committee architecture · secondary use of health data · hospital exemption with sanctions · a new levy or agency. An MAH may commission an **evidence annex**. It does not become a fictitious sponsor.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

The parliamentary-bill mode is the highest-level production mode. It exists because health reform often discovers, in COMPLY or ANALYZE, that the missing authority is statutory. Hospital exemption with criminal sanctions, a new professional title, a new levy, a new agency, a rewrite of a founding health-professions statute, a comprehensive clinical-trial and ethics-committee law, or a secondary-use-of-health-data statute are not regulation work.

The general justification of a member bill is a political-legal document. The protocol’s template asks it to cover the importance of the subject, the constitutional frame, the treaty frame, Union alignment where claimed, at least two EU-member and one further peer, high-court signals, doctrine, expected effects on patients and institutions, and a budget summary. That is the file a committee can send onward. Article justification remains non-repetitive: it explains each article’s reason.

The signature block is a blank for real members. Cureolex does not invent sponsors, party labels, or a fictitious motion number. It does not put Roche, or any MAH, on the signature line. The committee-referral note is a reasoned suggestion, not a speakership decision. Internal-rules points (who may complete a deficient bill, how the language of the constitution and of statutes is checked) are stated so that the proposing office is not surprised later.

Industry picture: a research-based company that wants a determinate ethics-committee clock, or a lawful secondary-use regime for real-world evidence, is asking for a **statute-shaped** file. Roche public-policy materials list personalised healthcare and access to data among engagement issues (Roche public materials, 2025 — public-policy page). The matching Cureolex artefact is a bill dossier or an evidence annex a member could attach — not a Roche data-access programme, and not a claim that the company should legislate. If the user actually needed a regulation, this mode is the wrong vehicle; the protocol should have sent them to DRAFT. The value of this slide is to keep that distinction visible in the room. In Turkey the vehicle is a TBMM kanun teklifi. If the designated country were Germany, the United Kingdom, or Japan, the dossier would follow Bundestag / Bundesrat, Westminster, or Diet introduction rules — the destination shape of the same function, with Turkey proving that a full bill package can already be assembled.

### What the function does

Assembles a constitution-and-standing-orders-compatible member-bill dossier for the designated parliament, without pretending that the executive form book governs member bills and without inventing industry sponsors.

### What the user walks away with

A **legislative-bill package**: cover and signatures (blank), general justification, bill text, article justification, procedure and referral note, annexes — for human sponsors and parliamentary services to process. An MAH walks away with an **annex they may offer**, not with a filed motion.

## Slide 14 — EX POST — evaluate a rule that already binds

### On-screen text

**When to use it**
The instrument has been in force, typically **12–36 months**, and someone must decide: keep, revise, or repeal.

**Five better-regulation tests** (OECD-style, in the designated country)

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Did it achieve the stated aim? |
| Efficiency | At what cost, to whom? |
| Coherence | Does it still fit the rest of the hierarchy? |
| Relevance | Is the problem still the problem? |
| Union / peer value | Did alignment deliver what was claimed? |

**The closing recommendation is technical, not political**

- **Keep** the rule and monitor
- **Revise** → hand off to AMEND
- **Sunset / repeal** → hand off to a repeal instrument or a parliamentary bill

**Pharma-stakeholder pictures:** an early-access or named-patient scheme · an additional-monitoring list · a SUT class line · a hospital-exemption reporting duty. Official statistics only. No brand utilisation series that the administration did not publish.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

EX POST is the look-back that better-regulation practice demands and that health files often skip. It is not a second DRAFT. It reconstructs what the original impact file (if any) promised, then asks what actually happened: implementation circulars, regulator list movements, gazette amendments, court challenges, stakeholder complaints, and — where they exist — official statistics. Clinical and utilisation metrics are used when the subject is a medicine or a pathway; they are labelled as evidence, not as a substitute for the legal tests.

The five OECD-style criteria are each given a reasoned finding. A rule can be effective and still incoherent (it works, but it now contradicts a later notice). It can be relevant and inefficient (the problem is real, the administrative cost is indefensible). The protocol will not invent a utilisation time series that the administration did not publish. Missing implementation data is reported as a finding: *we cannot score efficiency because the official series was not available*. An MAH must not feed unpublished trial results or brand sales into this report and expect them to become official findings.

The keep / revise / repeal recommendation is explicitly a **technical proposal**. The political decision stays with parliament, the ministry, the regulator, the payer, or the head of government, as competence in that country requires. If the recommendation is “revise”, the natural next file is AMEND. If the recommendation is “repeal a statutory duty”, the next file is a parliamentary bill.

Worked picture (Turkey): a TİTCK scheduling tebliğ, thirty months on. Did the list stay current? Did firms and pharmacies know how to read it? Did a later kanun silently occupy the field? The evaluation report answers those questions and, if needed, attaches a revision roadmap. Second picture: an early-access or additional-monitoring instrument. Did designated centres actually open? Did the list vintage drift from the gazette rule? Did a kılavuz quietly replace the tebliğ? The same five tests apply if the living instrument is a German ordinance, a UK statutory instrument, or a Japanese ministerial notice. Roche public access materials speak of bringing medicines and diagnostics to patients faster and more equitably (Roche public materials, 2025–2026 — strategy / access). Cureolex will not score a Turkish tebliğ against a Roche access goal. It will score the tebliğ against **its own stated aim** and against official series.

### What the function does

Measures a living instrument against its own aims and against better-regulation criteria, then proposes keep / revise / repeal.

### What the user walks away with

An **ex-post evaluation report**: identity of the instrument, ex-ante versus actual table, five-criteria findings, court and stakeholder map, technical recommendation, and a corrective roadmap.

### On-screen text

A file for a **designated country** tries to attach, from official channels:

| Layer | What it is for |
|---|---|

| **Parliament history** | Bills, committee reports, and the trail that a current article came from |

| **Doctrine** | Checkable academic writing of that country (and, where lawful, foreign doctrine) |

If a source cannot be read, the file says so and gives the portal path. Absence of a hit is not proof that a rule does not exist.

**Coverage is uneven.** Turkey currently fills this table as a complete national stack. Other countries fill it through official legislation portals and the international shelf — deeply enough for landscape, comparative, and impact work; not yet equally for a full nine-mode draft in every legislative idiom.

**Industry reading:** the kılavuz, the additional-monitoring list, and the reimbursement annex are often the rows that decide a görüş. They are also the rows that go stale. Vintage is stated.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

A health reform that only reads “the famous statute” will miss the notice that actually binds the pharmacist, the circular that the inspector applies, and the committee report that explains why the statute article was written that way. Cureolex is built to read that whole official stack **in the country that was named**. A research-based MAH that only reads the gazette will miss the TİTCK kılavuz that the inspector is already applying. ANALYZE exists for that miss.

The table on this slide is the protocol’s ambition for every country file. It is also the honesty device. Not every row is live at the same depth in every jurisdiction. Official gazettes and consolidations differ in what they expose; some regulators publish a product index, others do not; some parliaments put committee reports in the open, others do not. The coverage statement at the end of a package is how that unevenness is shown, row by row, instead of being smoothed into a false completeness.

Treaties and ICH are not “foreign decoration.” They travel with the designated country: the question is whether *this* State is a party, and whether *this* regulator already treats an ICH text as the scientific floor. OECD series are used in RIA when a number is needed and an official series exists. Doctrine supports a justification or an opinion; it is never a substitute for the official text of a statute. Roche public reliance materials treat ICH and WHO good-reliance practice as the trust floor between regulators (Roche public materials, 2024–2025). That is a reason to put ICH on this shelf. It is not a reason to treat a Roche position paper as a source of law.

### What the function does

States the source architecture that every country file is supposed to attach.

### What the user walks away with

A **source map** they can use to brief a project: which layers the protocol will try to read, and which gaps must be declared rather than guessed.

## Slide 16 — Turkey example — the deepest national stack

### On-screen text

### Speaker notes / full meaning

This slide is the worked example of Slide 15, not a second product identity. Turkey is where the protocol can already run the full official stack: gazette, consolidated law, parliament history, ministry soft law, current institution names, and the medicines regulator’s public index.

Resmî Gazete is used for publication facts: the date, the issue number, the text as printed. Historical dating on the Gazette must be handled with care (see the limits slide). The legislation corpus is used for the living consolidated text, the article tree, earlier versions, and the official gerekçe when it is on file. TBMM sources are used when the question is “how did this article enter the law?” — essential for a 1219 reform or for any bill that amends a much-amended statute.

Ministry of Health materials that never appear in the Gazette are not treated as inferior decoration. In Turkish health administration they often *are* the working rule. The protocol reads them, labels them as soft law, and will not pretend that a kılavuz has the rank of a yönetmelik. That is the ANALYZE finding an MAH most often needs before a görüş. The organisation registry is a small function with a large effect: drafts that still say a directorate that was abolished by Presidential Decree No. 4 fail both form and substance.

The index is used to see **which class** a rule will land on — not to write a Roche brand into the article. Pricing, scheduling, and similar lists are administrative snapshots. They go stale. EUDAMED can still inform a comparative chapter about Union devices, with the maturity caveats that database itself carries.

If both official channels fail, the protocol will not fabricate a Turkish article. It will say the official text could not be confirmed. That is the same epistemic rule the protocol will apply when another country is named.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

This is the international shelf that COMPARATIVE LAW, RIA, DRAFT justification, and parliamentary justification draw on. The protocol prefers official legislation sites over commentary. For the United States that means the federal health corpus and the Federal Register; for Japan and Australia, the official e-legislation services; for Spain and Ireland, the official gazettes and statute books; for Canada, the federal justice consolidation; for China and Mexico, the official recent-law channels, with a best-effort caveat.

Union law is handled as *existence and type* first: is there a regulation or a directive, what is its date, is a consolidated version claimed? That is enough to stop a justification from citing a number that was never enacted. The full article text of a Union act is used when it can be confirmed; otherwise the official Union portal is given. German law is the usual implementing peer for medicines and devices — including hospital exemption and social-code listing. Japanese law is the usual second peer when regenerative medicine or NHI listing is the question.

Treaties are not decorative. In a Turkish file, under Anayasa md. 90, a duly in-force treaty on fundamental rights and freedoms prevails in a conflict with statute. In another designated country the same lookup is done against **that** constitution’s treaty rule. ICESCR Article 12, the Oviedo Convention, and MEDICRIME are typical health-adjacent instruments the protocol will actually look up (status, reservations, Council of Europe signature picture where a captured official snapshot exists). It will not invent a ratification date.

ICH guidelines are the quality, safety, efficacy, and multidisciplinary texts that medicines agencies already treat as the scientific floor. They are cited as guidelines, not as domestic law, and not as an eCTD file-format manual. OECD series are used in RIA when a number is needed and an official series exists. Roche public reliance materials put ICH and WHO good-reliance practice on the same trust shelf (Roche public materials, 2024–2025). Cureolex will cite the **ICH or WHO text**, not the Roche story, as the benchmark.

The honest present tense: these official portals already let another country be the **file’s home** for ANALYZE, COMPARATIVE LAW, and RIA. They do not yet make every jurisdiction an equal DRAFT/AMEND/COMPLY/OPINE/parliamentary-bill production language. That is the aim this deck is about.

## Slide 23 — A typical journey — from country and topic to sign-off

### On-screen text

**A country and a topic in**
“Designated country: Turkey. We need an ATMP regime.” · “This off-label circular is unusable.” · “Amend the reimbursement line.” · “TİTCK opened a kılavuz; the affiliate has a comment window.”

**Then, in order**

1. **Scope** — is this reform, or a lawsuit / promotion file / a filing? If reform, which legal level in **this** country? Who is speaking — ministry, regulator, or MAH stakeholder?
2. **ANALYZE** — what already exists, including unpublished ministry and TİTCK material?
3. **COMPARATIVE LAW** — what do the Union, a named peer, ICH, and the treaties actually require or suggest?
4. **DRAFT or AMEND** — write the instrument in this country’s form, with both justification layers *(ministry/regulator)*; or propose a labelled delta *(MAH)*
5. **COMPLY** — fitness against **this** country’s drafting rules; repair the fails
6. **RIA** — if the vehicle is a statute or high-level decree, or the budget effect is material
7. **OPINE** — if the file must now be answered *as* an institution or as a consulted stakeholder
8. **Package** — operative text, justification, tables, annexes, coverage, confidence
9. **Human sign-off** — counsel of record (for an MAH: affiliate / Global legal), authorised manager, then official process

**Later:** EX POST, 12–36 months after force.

Modes that the vehicle does not need may be skipped. A clean phrase-change may be AMEND plus COMPLY. A kılavuz comment window may be ANALYZE plus OPINE.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

This is the journey to commission, not a mandatory waterfall. A member who already knows the gap may start at the parliamentary-bill mode. A centre-of-government file that arrives for opinion starts at OPINE, with ANALYZE in the background. An MAH whose kılavuz arrived yesterday starts at ANALYZE and OPINE, not at DRAFT of a yönetmelik. The first fact in the brief is the **country**. Without it, the protocol cannot choose a gazette, a form book, or a parliament. The second fact, when industry is in the room, is **who is speaking**.

The ATMP example shows the full chain in Turkey: this is a product-class regime, not a single-patient compassionate-use petition. Analyze: 1262 / 3359 / 7223 and Presidential Decree No. 4, the current ruhsat yönetmelik, any TİTCK ATMP kılavuz, silence or presence in SUT. Comparative: Union ATMP regulation, German implementing law, ICH gene-therapy safety texts, Oviedo if human-substance rules are touched. Draft: a yönetmelik in 5210 md. 15 order, hospital exemption only if the parent law can carry it. Comply: no invented penalties, determinate timelines, alphabetical definitions. RIA if the vehicle is raised to kanun or CBK, or if designated-centre costs are the real decision. The package then goes to a human. Twelve to thirty-six months later, EX POST asks whether designated centres actually opened and whether patients reached product.

The off-label example is usually ANALYZE then AMEND or DRAFT of a circular or notice, with regulator-list honesty and a certainty repair. The reimbursement example is almost always AMEND plus a comparison table, with clinical evidence labelled as evidence and budget cost flagged for the public payer. The kılavuz-window example is the Roche-flavoured path: ANALYZE the unpublished stack, COMPLY the draft if it is already in article form, OPINE inside the clock that actually applies, attach a proposed comparison table if a SUT or tebliğ delta is the ask. If the same journey were commissioned for Germany, the United Kingdom, or Japan, steps 1–3 and 6 would already have a natural official-portal home; steps 4–5, 7, and the parliamentary dossier are the destination in that legislative idiom.

Shows how the nine modes compose into one institutional project in a named country, including when the commissioner is a consulted MAH.

If the political wish is a new offence or a new agency, the file should change vehicle to a parliamentary bill rather than overload the regulation. Hospital exemption is the industry-sensitive article: who may manufacture, for which named patient, with what reporting, and whether the exemption product later seeks reimbursement. In the Turkey illustration, that is a TİTCK-facing yönetmelik under 1262 / 3359 / 7223. Devices that travel with an ATMP raise EUDAMED for the Union side and a hard stop on ÜTS for the Turkish side. In Germany, the United Kingdom, or Japan the competent bodies and the statute/ordinance split would change; the modes would not. A Roche affiliate commissioning this file is asking for a **class regime**, not for authorisation of a named Roche ATMP.

Off-label use is a classic certainty problem. The scientific fact (a product used outside its approved particulars) is easy. The legal fact (who may authorise, for which patient class, with what registry, with what reimbursement consequence) is usually scattered across a circular, a regulator note, and a payer silence. ANALYZE makes that scatter visible. DRAFT or AMEND then replaces “as deemed appropriate” with a board, a period, and a report. OPINE is how the regulator answers if the ministry sent the draft out — and how an MAH answers if they are the consulted stakeholder. Turkey’s names for that scatter are TİTCK notes, a Sağlık Bakanlığı genelge, and SUT silence. Compassionate, named-patient, and early-access schemes belong on the same map; they are not synonyms for off-label, and they are not promotional named-patient programmes.

Reimbursement is a classic AMEND. In Turkey the living text is the SUT of 24 March 2013 with a long amendment chain. The comparison table is the product. Acquired-rights and non-retroactivity matter: a line that cuts an ongoing treatment needs a transitional article, not only a new annex row. Clinical papers support the justification; they do not become the legal text. When the labelled use requires a companion diagnostic, the class line and the test belong in the same delta — that is the personalised-healthcare theme mapped onto an artefact, not a Roche product claim. The German, British, or Japanese analogue is a social-code / G-BA, NHS–NICE / tariff, or NHI listing delta — same artefact type, local instrument.

### What the function does

Shows the same protocol producing three different, realistic artefacts, with Turkey as illustration, other countries as the same problem in a different house, and the MAH as a possible commissioner of the comment pack.

### What the user walks away with

Three **sample commissions** they can copy into a request: ATMP package, off-label circular, reimbursement delta — always with the country named first.

Originator and biosimilar products live under the same hierarchy. ## Slide 27 — How to start a file tomorrow

### On-screen text

**Bring**

1. The **country** (the designated jurisdiction — not assumed)
2. The **topic** in one sentence (the patient group, the product class, or the broken article)
3. The **legal vehicle you think you need** — or the honest statement “we do not know if this is a notice, a regulation, or a statute”
4. Any **draft already in the building**, even if rough
5. The **institution that must sign** — ministry, regulator, parliament, association, or MAH affiliate function
6. The **deadline** (especially a consultation clock)
7. If industry: confirmation that this is a **norm file**, not a leave-behind, a filing, or a price

**Ask for, by name**

- a landscape note · a draft · an amendment table · a fitness report against this country’s form book · an opinion letter · an impact annex · a comparative report · a parliamentary-bill dossier · an ex-post evaluation

**Expect back**
The artefact + coverage (including how deep this country could be read) + confidence + a list of what a human must still confirm on an official portal.

**Then**
Counsel of record reviews — for a research-based MAH, affiliate and, where required, Global legal. The official process — consultation, centre of government, parliament, gazette — remains the State’s.

### Speaker notes / full meaning

This closing slide is operational. Cureolex starts from a country and a sentence, not from a theory of health law. The most valuable thing a user can say, after the country and the topic, is the uncertainty: “we do not know the legal level.” That is ANALYZE first, not DRAFT first. The second most valuable thing is an existing draft: COMPLY and OPINE are faster and more useful when they can attack a real text. The third, when the commissioner is Roche or another MAH, is the clock and the voice: who is speaking, and by when must the görüş exist.

The consultation clock should be in the first message when the user is the consulted body. In the Turkey example, silence can be assent for 5210 consultees; a beautiful letter on day 16 is a political problem. A kılavuz window may be shorter or differently counted; name it. The signatory’s name matters because OPINE is written in an institutional voice. A Roche affiliate letter is not a TİTCK letter.

What should not be in the first message: a request for a lawsuit, a request for advertising copy, a request to “prove that no such rule exists” from an empty search, a request to publish, a request to file with TİTCK or EMA, a request to set a price, a request to guarantee a SUT line, or a request to treat every named country as if it already had Turkey’s depth.

The last sentence the room should hear is the same sentence on the first slide. Cureolex produces a file for human sign-off in the country that was named. A research-based company may commission that file. The State publishes.
