# JARS — APA Journal Article Reporting Standards (quantitative)

Use JARS-Quant for general quantitative empirical reports when no design-
specific guideline (CONSORT/STROBE) fits better. Report present / missing /
partial with locating text. Source: APA Publications and Communications Board
Working Group; Appelbaum M et al. Am Psychol 2018;73:3.

- **Title/Abstract** — variables and design identifiable; abstract states problem,
  participants, method, findings, conclusions.
- **Introduction** — problem, prior work, hypotheses with rationale, and whether
  each is primary/secondary/exploratory.
- **Method** — participant characteristics + eligibility; sampling procedures and
  settings; sample size, power, and precision (a priori determination);
  measures/instruments with psychometrics; research design; manipulations/conditions.
- **Results** — participant flow and recruitment dates; missing data + how handled;
  for each hypothesis: the analysis, the test statistic with df, the exact p (or
  a bound), and an effect size with a confidence interval; assumptions checked;
  no selective reporting of outcomes.
- **Discussion** — support for each hypothesis; limitations; constraints on
  generality; implications.
- **Other** — registration; open-data/materials/code statement; conflicts; funding.

Red flags for LLM text: a p-value with no effect size or CI; "significant"
without a test statistic; no distinction between confirmatory and exploratory
analyses; sample size with no power justification.
